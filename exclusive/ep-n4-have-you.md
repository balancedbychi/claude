# EXCLUSIVE — the new order
## Episode 4: "Have You"

**Status: PROPOSED — nothing locked, nothing spent.** Read
`exclusive/new-order-README.md` first. This episode does not work without it.

**Runtime:** 1:40 · 7 clips · 100 seconds
**Format:** One short scene in a café, then one room for the rest. Night.
**Logline:** Simone tells Nia, kindly, which is worse. Nia goes to Chi's apartment.
Chi confirms every clause without defending one of them, and gets slapped on the
word "twelve."

---

## THE TITLE

**"Have you."** Dorian, prologue Episode 2, Clip 2 — *"You must be Chi. I've heard
a lot." / "Have you."* Two people performing a first meeting in front of a woman
they were both lying to, and the whole season was hiding inside a two-word
deflection.

**The words are never spoken in this episode.** If a line has to point at the
retcon, the line is wrong. The title does it and the title is enough.

---

## WHAT HAS TO HAPPEN AND WHAT MUST NOT

**Must happen:** Chi tells the truth completely, unprompted, without being asked
the right question, and without one word of defence. She has been not-saying it for
three weeks and when it finally comes out it comes out whole.

**Must not happen — and each of these is the obvious version:**

- **Chi must not apologise.** Not once, not in any phrasing. "I'm sorry" makes the
  scene about whether Nia forgives her, and that is a smaller scene.
- **Chi must not explain while she is being accused.** In C3 she has ten words and
  all of them are Nia's own words repeated back. She does not get to start
  explaining until after she has been hit.
- **Nia must not forgive her.** No reconciliation, no hug, no "I just need time."
  She leaves. The last thing she gives Chi is an "okay" that decides nothing.
- **Nobody moralises and nobody is punished.** The show has no opinion. **This is
  the episode where breaking that rule would be easiest and most fatal.**
- **No music cue on the slap.** No sting, no swell, no drop-out-to-silence effect.

### The one rule this season breaks, and it breaks here

**"Chi gets fewer words than Nia, always."** Clip 5 is the longest speech Chi has
had in seven episodes — 78 words, uninterrupted, and Nia does not speak once during
it.

**Every bit of its power is borrowed from the rule being absolute everywhere
else.** She has 3 words in C2, 10 in C3, 26 in C4 and 2 in C6 — and then 71 in one
unbroken run. **Her longest single line in N1 through N3 is nine words.** That is
the invariant to enforce, and it is enforceable: no line of Chi's in the first
three episodes may run past nine. **If she gets a paragraph anywhere else in this
season, Clip 5 is worth nothing.**

## THE SCRIPT

Seven clips, 100 seconds. C1 is `Sucré-Coffee-Shop`, day. **C2 through C7 are one
unbroken scene in `ChiChi-Living-Room`, at night** — the room from the very first
shot of the series, where she sat with one glass and one candle and nobody.

50mm throughout except C5. Cuts land on the first syllable. **Three holds: the beat
before "Baby" in C1, the beat before "No" in C6, and all of C7.**

### CLIP 01 — "Baby" · 13s · 50mm · f/2.2 · two cuts
**Int. Sucré — day.** The same table, and that is deliberate — Nia is sitting where
she threw the water. Simone across from her, cheerful, two coffees, having a
perfectly nice time.

> **SIMONE:** I'm going to enjoy this more than I should. I want you to know I know
> that.
> **NIA:** Just say it.
> **SIMONE:** Four months. Two years before you. He never got his key back.
> **NIA:** ...Who?
> *(one beat — and Simone's whole face goes soft, almost tender)*
> **SIMONE:** Baby.

*⚠️ **"Baby" is Chi's word for Nia** — it is what Chi calls her in the prologue when
she is being gentle with her. **Simone says it in exactly that register, and it
identifies Chi without a name.** Nia hears the word before she hears the meaning.
That is the whole clip and it costs five letters.*

*⚠️ **Simone is not cruel here and the episode falls apart if she is.** She warns
Nia first. She is honest about enjoying it, which is more than anyone else in this
season manages about anything. And when she lands it she is **kind** — soft voice,
soft face, no triumph, no smile, no tilt of the head. **Being kind about it is the
cruellest available choice and the model will fight you for a smirk on every take.**
Name and negate: smirk, satisfaction, triumph, raised eyebrow, head tilt, slow
blink, leaning back, a laugh.*

*"He never got his key back" is the hook for C7 and it is dropped in passing, as an
aside, as the least important of the three facts. **No stress on it. No push-in.**
It should be almost missable, and the audience should only understand what it was
for in the last six seconds of the season.*

*Nia's "...Who?" is not a real question. **She already knows.** The ellipsis is the
half-second where she runs the list and finds only one name on it.*

### CLIP 02 — "Okay" · 10s · 50mm · f/2.0 · one cut
**Int. Chi's living room — night.** `ChiChi-Living-Room`. The door. Chi opens it.
One glass of red already on the marble table behind her, one candle lit.

She takes one look at Nia's face and she knows everything instantly.

> **CHI:** Okay.
> **NIA:** "Okay"?
> **CHI:** Come in.

She steps back to let her past. **She does not ask what is wrong.** She does not say
"what happened." She does not look surprised for a single frame.

*⚠️ **Chi knowing on sight is the most efficient piece of characterisation in the
season.** She has been waiting three weeks for someone to arrive at this door
wearing that face, and when it happens her whole body just accepts it. **Play it as
relief.** It is over. She does not have to carry it any more, and the thing she
feels first — before the fear — is that.*

*"Okay." here rhymes with Kel's "I said okay" in N2 C6 and it means something
completely different: **his was withdrawal, hers is surrender.** Same word, two
episodes apart, opposite events. Nobody remarks on it.*

*Nia repeating it back is the first of three times in this episode she gets Chi's
own words handed to her, and the only one that is not deliberate on Chi's part.*

### CLIP 03 — "I Already Knew" · 15s · 50mm · f/2.0 · three cuts
**Same.** They are standing. **Neither of them sits down at any point in this
episode** — in a room built entirely around a sofa, which is the whole visual idea.

> **NIA:** Four months.
> **CHI:** Four months.
> **NIA:** Before.
> **CHI:** Two years before.
> **NIA:** And on the phone. When I said his name. On the phone, Chi — you said
> *"who is he."*
> **CHI:** I did.
> **NIA:** You already knew.
> **CHI:** I already knew.

*⚠️ **Chi's technique here is to give Nia her own sentences back, flat, confirmed,
with nothing added** — and it is the most damning performance available. No
qualification, no "it wasn't like that," no context, no "let me explain." Every
accusation is simply true and she says so.*

*It is also, quietly, the same move she has used on everyone all season: **answer
truthfully and incompletely.** Here there is nothing left to leave out, so the
technique has nowhere to go, and what it produces is a confession that sounds like
an echo.*

*"Two years before" is Chi **correcting Nia upward** — volunteering a worse fact
than the one she was accused of. She does that twice in this clip. **It is the
closest thing to an apology she offers and it is not one.***

*Nia quoting "who is he" is the fourth and last instance of the season's quotation
habit: Chi quoted "eleven" at Nia, Simone picked up "either" from Dorian, Nia quoted
"here" at Chi, and now Nia quotes **the prologue's own dialogue** at her. Light
stress on the quotation, as written.*

*Chi has 10 words here to Nia's 25, and her longest line in the clip is four.
**Hold that.** It is the last clip in which the economy rule holds cleanly.*

### CLIP 04 — "Twelve" · 15s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts · **THE SLAP**
**Same.**

> **NIA:** Say something that isn't my own sentence back at me.
> *(beat)*
> **CHI:** I had about four seconds to say it on that phone call and I didn't. And
> then it was eleven days—
> **NIA:** Don't.
> **CHI:** —and then it was twelve—

**SLAP.** On the word **"twelve."**

*⚠️ **This is the best beat in the four scripts and everything in the season bends
to protect it.** The counting has run through six episodes as a joke — eleven days,
Chi correcting it to twelve, Nia mocking her for keeping score, twenty-one
volunteered unprompted. **It has never once been a joke. It has been a woman
counting her own silence and being laughed at for it.** It detonates on a slap. Do
not let any note soften the landing word.*

*⚠️ **NEVER RENDER THE CONTACT.** Two character elements touching is where faces
break, and an impact frame is the worst instance of it available. **Cut on the
windup** — Nia's hand coming up, at the top of its arc, before contact. **The sound
lands over the cut.** C5 opens on the aftermath, which is a still, held state and
costs nothing.*

*Name and negate in the prompt: hands meeting, contact, impact, a face deforming, a
hand across a cheek, two figures overlapping, motion blur across a face, any frame
in which the hand and the head occupy the same space.*

*⚠️ **"Don't." is Nia asking her to stop, and Chi does not stop.** That is why she
gets hit. She is not being defiant; she has waited three weeks to say this and she
physically cannot stop halfway through. **The slap interrupts a confession, it does
not answer an insult** — which is what makes it the most sympathetic slap available
and also completely deserved. The show has no opinion on that and neither should the
staging.*

*Chi has 26 words here to Nia's 11 — **the first clip in the whole season where
she outspeaks her scene partner.** The rule is already bending one clip early, on
purpose, so C5 does not arrive from nowhere.*

### CLIP 05 — "The Counting" · 25s · 75mm → 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts
**Same.** Opens tight on Chi. **Her head is still turned from the slap. One cheek
darker. She has not moved. She does not touch her face — not once, not at any point
in the clip.** She turns her head back, slowly, and starts talking.

Pull back to 50mm across the speech, so she gets smaller in the room as it goes on.

> **CHI:** I wasn't counting your days.
> *(beat)*
> **CHI:** You said eleven. I said twelve, and you laughed at me for keeping score.
> I wasn't keeping score on you. I was counting how long I'd gone without telling
> you. Twelve days. Then twenty-one. Then I couldn't, because the not-saying had
> got bigger than the thing.
> *(beat)*
> **CHI:** I told myself that was protecting you. It was protecting the part of me
> that had already decided not to.

**Nia does not speak, react, interrupt or move for the entire clip.**

*⚠️ **The one broken rule of the season, and it is only worth something because of
how hard the rule was held everywhere else.** 78 words. Uninterrupted. The woman
whose entire power is that she can end an argument with "Mm."*

*⚠️ **She is not performing and she is not pleading.** Same voice she has always
had: level, unhurried, dry, accurate. **She is doing what she does to other
people's lives, to her own, out loud, for the first time** — and the diagnosis is
merciless because it is hers. This is CLAUDE.md §1's blind-spot rule paying out in
full: she reads everyone clearly and herself terribly, and here she finally reads
herself and it is the worst news she has ever delivered.*

*⚠️ **The last two sentences are the season.** "I told myself that was protecting
you. It was protecting the part of me that had already decided not to." No emphasis,
no pause before it, no swallow. She says it the way she said "It's a queue."*

*The pull-back is the only camera move in the episode and it is almost
imperceptible. **She gets smaller. The room stays the same size.** Do not push in on
her face at the end — the instinct is to, and it makes the speech about her pain
rather than about what she is saying.*

*⚠️ **Not touching her face is the single most important physical note in the
clip.** Every take will offer a hand to the cheek. It must never arrive. She took
the water without wiping it and she takes this without touching it, and the second
time it is not composure any more — **it is that she does not think she is allowed
to.** Same gesture, opposite meaning, and that is the rhyme the water was planted
for.*

### CLIP 06 — "Look at Me" · 12s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts
**Same.** Both in frame. The distance between them has not changed since C3.

> **NIA:** Do you still want him?
> *(and this is the only time all season that Chi hesitates)*
> **CHI:** No.
> **NIA:** Look at me.
> *(Chi looks at her.)*
> **CHI:** No.
> **NIA:** ...Okay.

Nia goes. The door. Chi does not follow her and does not say her name.

*⚠️ **The hesitation before the first "No" is the last piece of juice in the season
and it is about half a second long.** She is not lying. **She is deciding whether
the true answer is simple enough to say in one word**, and it is not, and she says
it anyway. That is the same crime she has committed for seven episodes, committed
one final time, in front of us, and Nia asks her to do it again properly.*

*The second "No" — looking straight at her — **is completely true.** She does not
want him. **What is in the drawer is not about wanting him**, and the episode does
not tell us what it is about, and neither does the next one until it is ready.*

*"...Okay." decides nothing. It is not forgiveness and it is not a verdict. It is
the sound of a woman with nowhere to put something. **Nia does not slam the door.**
She closes it normally, which is much worse.*

*⚠️ **Chi does not go after her, does not say "Nia", does not reach.** She has said
everything she has. End the clip the moment the door is shut.*

### CLIP 07 — "The Drawer" · 10s · 50mm · f/2.2 · locked static · **NO DIALOGUE**
**Int. Chi's living room — night.** The room from the first shot of the series.

Chi alone. **One glass of red on the marble table. One candle. Nobody.** She stands
in it for a moment.

Then she crosses to a drawer, opens it, and takes out **a key.**

She looks at it. She closes the drawer. **She puts the key in her pocket.**

Hold three seconds. Cut to black.

*⚠️ **This is the season ending and it is ten wordless seconds.** It is the exact
composition of prologue Episode 1, Shot 2 — *"the apartment is beautiful and very
quiet," one glass, one candle, nobody* — and it is also the bookend of the
prologue's own last shot, where she waited alone for a lift and looked at a phone
with nothing on it. **Same woman, same room, same emptiness, one new object.***

*⚠️ **Declare the silence:** "this clip is the exception, there is no dialogue, the
silence is the point, do not fill it, no music, no score, no swell, no voice, no
third person in the room." The swell under a final hold has beaten hard negation
twice — **budget an ffmpeg duck on the tail and do not spend a take fighting it.***

*⚠️ **Play the key completely flat.** No lingering look, no close-up on it, no turn
to camera, no small smile, no tightening of the hand. She takes it out, she looks
at it for about a second, she pockets it. **The audience does the rest, which is the
only thing this show has ever asked of them.***

*She is not going to see him. She is not not going to see him. **That is the hook
and it stays a hook** — do not resolve it here and do not let a note add a beat that
tells us which.*

## CONTINUITY

- **Chi's look: the series look is strongly recommended** — oversized heather-oatmeal
  PILATES crewneck, chocolate-brown leggings, barefoot, stud earrings only. It is
  already locked, it costs nothing, **and it is what she was wearing in the first
  shot of the series.** The finale is a bookend and the wardrobe should say so
  without anyone mentioning it.
- **Chi wears NO RING, ever** — named object by object, including nothing on the
  fourth finger of her left hand. ⚠️ **Non-negotiable in this episode above all
  others**: she is the other woman, the scene is about a man, and a wedding ring
  destroys the premise rather than merely breaking continuity. Gold watch only.
  Nails deep red.
- **Chi's hair sweeps to HER OWN RIGHT** — deep side part on the LEFT of her head,
  volume falling down her right side; **facing camera, screen LEFT.** Never centre,
  never her left, never symmetrical, never tied back.
- **Clothes adapt to Chi's body, never the reverse** — tailored realistically to her
  real full-figured proportions.
- ⚠️ **The mark on Chi's cheek: one side, subtle, consistent.** Present from the
  first frame of C5 and **identical in C6 and C7.** Name the side and pin it in all
  three prompts. It does not spread, darken or fade between clips. It is never
  touched, never examined, never mentioned by anyone.
- **Nia's neckline never changes between clips.** Choker only if the reference
  leaves her throat bare, **never on top of fabric.** Gold ring, same hand, same
  finger.
- **Two characters per scene and no more.** C1 is Simone and Nia, and **Dorian is
  not in this episode at all** — he is discussed in every clip and appears in none,
  which is the point. No background figures in the apartment. Sparse soft background
  in the café only.
- **Night key in the apartment: warm lamps and one candle only.** Never overheads,
  never daylight, never blue-dominant, never coloured. C1 is daylight, warm and
  natural, matching N3 exactly.
- **The marble coffee table is the locked one** — one low RECTANGULAR table, thick
  pale white-and-grey veined top, solid marble base, directly in front of the sofa.
  Never round, oval, glass, wood or metal; never a second table in frame.
- **One wine glass.** Pin the count explicitly — "a single glass" has historically
  not been strong enough phrasing. A second glass implies company that is not there
  and it is the entire meaning of the last shot.

### The word counts, and exactly where the rule breaks

**Chi 112 · Nia 50 · Simone 30.** ⚠️ **For the first time in seven episodes, Chi
outspeaks the room over a whole episode** — and that inversion IS the event, so it
is worth being precise about where it happens rather than waving at it.

| | Chi | the other person | rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | — | Simone 30 / Nia 4 | Chi not present |
| C02 | 3 | Nia 1 | trivial, both monosyllabic |
| C03 | 10 | Nia 25 | ✅ holds |
| C04 | 26 | Nia 11 | ⚠️ **bends** — the confession starts |
| C05 | **71** | Nia **0** | 🚨 **breaks** |
| C06 | 2 | Nia 9 | ✅ holds, immediately |

**The measurable invariant across N1–N3 is not the per-clip count — it is that Chi
never gets a speech.** Her longest single line in the first three episodes is
**nine words** ("A lot of women have been in this kitchen", N2 C5). In C05 she has
seventy-one, uninterrupted. **That is a jump of nearly eight to one and it is the
only reason the clip works.**

So the rule to enforce is exact: **no line of Chi's in N1–N3 may exceed nine
words**, and she snaps back to two words in C06 the moment the speech ends. If any
take in the earlier episodes gives her a paragraph, C05 is worth nothing.

**Do not give Clip 5 a per-line time budget.** It is precisely the shape of thing
that backfired in the prologue — the protected line came back the *fastest* in its
clip and the model banked the surplus as silence elsewhere. **Size the container at
25s and let it fall.**
