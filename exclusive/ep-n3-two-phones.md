**Status: PROPOSED — nothing locked, nothing spent.** Revised 16 Sep 2026. Read
`exclusive/new-order-README.md` first.

**Runtime:** 1:44 · 6 clips · 104 seconds
**Format:** One set, one table, one afternoon. The table format. Daytime.
**Logline:** Nia is running two men and calls it parity. A lawyer at the next table

## WHAT THIS EPISODE IS

**Nia's villain stretch and Chi's blind spot arrive on the same afternoon at the
same table.** Two stories, one set, both women losing something in front of a
stranger who turns out to be the most interesting man in the room.

### Nia, plainly

She is seeing Dorian and she is seeing Elias, and she has a theory: *"nobody's the
idiot."* The theory is funny, internally consistent, delivered fast, and wrong, and
Chi refutes it in three words. Then Nia does the genuinely villainous thing — **she
has texted Simone to the café to have it out in public** — and throws a glass of
water in her face. **The show has no opinion.** She is not a fool and she is not
punished. She is out of it by N4.

### Chi, plainly

Graham is at the next table. He is precise, dry, reads the room correctly, and
laughs at "it's a queue." Nia clocks the attraction in half a second. **For the
first time in the series Nia is the one grilling a man on Chi's behalf**, and she
is good at it. Then, alone, he asks her to dinner, she says she is seeing someone,
he asks "Are you?", and she has to admit she and Kel haven't said. And he tells her
he is separated.

**Chi has told Nia a hundred times that separated is married.** She picks up his
card. **That is the engine applied to Chi and it is her season.**

### Graham

44, white, successful, a lawyer. **Not a saviour, not a creep, not a tourist.** His
read on the water is "I'd take that case — hers," meaning Simone's, meaning he saw
exactly who was wronged, which is what Chi saw. **Shared accuracy is the
attraction.** His unavailability is the ordinary kind and he volunteers it on the
first afternoon, which is more than Dorian has ever done.

### Elias
**Never seen, never heard.** He is one sentence of Nia's. Keep him there.

### CLIP 01 — "Six-Minute Increments" · 16s · 50mm · f/2.2 · two cuts
**Int. Sucré — day.** Chi at her table, two cups already there — she ordered ahead,
as always. Phone at her ear. **Graham at the next table**, laptop open, jacket on
the chair, not looking up.

> **CHI** *(into the phone)*: You're late.

She hangs up. Checks her watch.

> **GRAHAM** *(not looking up)*: I bill in six-minute increments. She's at nine.
> **CHI** *(looks at him)*: Do people find that charming?
> **GRAHAM:** No. *(looks up)* But they remember it.
> **CHI:** Mm.
> **GRAHAM:** Graham.
> **CHI:** I didn't ask.
> **GRAHAM:** I know. I'm working on it.

*⚠️ **Nia is not on screen and does not speak** — "You're late" is the whole call.
An off-camera speaker gets an invented voice; Nia gets no line until she is at the
table.*

*"Do people find that charming?" is Chi's opening test and **"No. But they remember
it."** passes it — he does not claim the line worked, he claims it stuck. **He is
dry the way she is dry.** That is the whole meet.*

*⚠️ **Chi says "Mm." to a stranger.** That is her sound and she has used it on
three people in seven episodes. Using it on him is the tell — she has already
decided something. Short, flat, and she looks back at her cup after it.*

*"I know. I'm working on it." — self-aware, not entitled. **Never "you will."** He
is not confident she will ask; he is telling her he intends to earn it.*

---

### CLIP 02 — "It's a Queue" · 17s · 50mm · f/2.0 · three cuts
**Same.** Nia arrives in a rush, sits, and puts **two phones face down on the
table, side by side.** She does not hide them.

> **CHI:** Whose is that?
> **NIA:** Mine. Both mine. Dorian has Simone, I have Elias, nobody's the idiot.
> That's not cheating, Chi. That's parity.
> **CHI:** Parity.
> **NIA:** It's maths.
> **CHI:** It's a queue.

*(Graham, next table, laughs once — short, involuntary. Nia turns.)*

> **NIA:** Who's this?
> **CHI:** Nobody.
> **GRAHAM:** Graham.
> **NIA** *(Chi, then him, then Chi)*: Oh my God.

*⚠️ **Nia's tell is length and she opens with twenty-one words in one breath.** Play
it fast, bright, and genuinely funny — she is enjoying the bit and the audience
should too. Nobody feels superior to her for one frame.*

*⚠️ **"It's a queue." is Chi's entire function in three words.** No emphasis, no
pleasure, and **she does not look up from her cup.** She is correcting a figure.*

*Graham's laugh is the sound of a man hearing his own kind of sentence. **One
laugh, short, then he tries to take it back.** Nia's "Oh my God" is her clocking,
in half a second, that Chi is interested — because Chi said "Nobody" a fraction
too fast.*

### CLIP 03 — "He's White, Chi" · 16s · 50mm · f/2.0 · three cuts
**Same.** Nia has turned her whole chair toward Graham. Chi has not moved.

> **NIA:** What do you do, Graham?
> **GRAHAM:** I'm a lawyer.
> **NIA** *(to Chi, not quietly)*: He's white, Chi.
> **CHI:** I have eyes, Nia.
> **GRAHAM:** I'm sitting right here.
> **NIA:** I know. That's why I said it.

*(Graham laughs — a real one, from the chest. Chi almost does.)*

> **NIA** *(to Chi)*: He laughs. Kel laughs?
> **CHI:** Kel cooks.
> **NIA:** That wasn't the question.

*⚠️ **This is the clip that lets both audiences laugh and lectures neither.** Nia
says the obvious thing out loud because that is what Nia does and it is the show's
value; Chi's "I have eyes" says it is not the point; Graham's "I'm sitting right
here" says he is a person and not a category; Nia's "That's why I said it" says she
knows, and she was talking to him too. **Four lines, nobody is a symbol, nobody is
small.** Play every one of them warm.*

*"Kel laughs?" / "Kel cooks." / "That wasn't the question." — **Nia doing Chi's
thing to Chi**, and landing it. Graham now knows there is a Kel. Chi's "Kel cooks"
is two words and it is a defence, which is the first time she has defended anything
this season.*

### CLIP 04 — "I Got Your Text" · 17s · 50mm · f/2.2 · two cuts
**Same.** Simone walks in — cheerful, unhurried — and sits down at their table
without asking. Third chair. Chi's face does not change. Nia's does.

> **SIMONE** *(sitting, bright)*: I got your text.
> **CHI:** What text.
> **NIA:** I texted her.
> **CHI:** You what.
> **NIA:** We're adults, Chi. We're having coffee.
> **SIMONE** *(to Chi, warm)*: Hey, Chi. *(to Nia)* You must be the one who texts.
> **NIA:** And you must be the one who waits.
> **SIMONE** *(pleasantly)*: Wednesdays, mostly.

*⚠️ **"I texted her" is Nia's villain move and it is the worst thing she does all
season** — she has staged a public confrontation and invited the other woman to it.
Chi's "You what." is two words, flat, and it is the closest she comes to anger in
the episode. **No raised voice from anyone at this table at any point.***

*"Hey, Chi" — they met at the party (N1 C5, "Take her home"). Warm, genuine. Simone
likes Chi. **Everybody likes Chi.***

*"You must be the one who waits" is Nia's best line of the season and Simone's
"Wednesdays, mostly" beats it without trying. **Simone never competes. She agrees
and adds a detail.** That is her whole method and it is why she wins every scene.*

### CLIP 05 — "Tuesday, Then" · 18s · 50mm → 75mm · f/2.0 · three cuts · **THE WATER**
**Same.**

> **NIA** *(standing)*: Say that again.
> **SIMONE:** Wednesdays. Sometimes Sat—

Nia's hand closes on her water glass. The arm comes up. **CUT ON THE UPSWING.**

**CUT TO: Simone, already wet.** Water off the jaw. One strand of hair stuck to her
cheek. A dark patch spreading on the shoulder. A puddle on the table. **She does not
move.** Then she wipes one eye with a knuckle, quite cheerfully.

> **SIMONE:** ...Tuesday, then.

Nia takes both phones and goes. Every head in the café is turned. Silence.

> **GRAHAM** *(to Chi, quietly)*: I'd take that case.
> **CHI:** Whose?
> **GRAHAM** *(a nod at Simone)*: Hers.

Chi laughs — she cannot help it. Simone, dripping, laughs too.

*⚠️ **NEVER RENDER THE WATER IN FLIGHT.** Cut on the arm's upswing, land on the wet
state. The wet face is a *state* — held, still — and states render. Do not ask for
a splash. Ask, item by item: water running off the jaw, the strand of hair, the
dark patch, the puddle, and absolute stillness.*

*⚠️ **"Tuesday, then." is Simone taking Tuesday too.** She has just been handed a
day. Cheerful, dry, wet. **The wronged party is winning and enjoying it and the
show has no opinion about that either.***

*⚠️ **"Hers." is the line that makes Chi like him.** He watched the whole thing and
his read is the accurate one — the woman who got the water is the one with the
case. **That is what Chi saw.** Shared accuracy. Her laugh is real and it is the
first time she has laughed at anything a man said since Kel's "I know."*

*Nobody intervenes. **No waiter, no bystander close-up, no reaction shot of a
stranger.** Heads turned in soft focus, and that is all.*

### CLIP 06 — "Separated" · 20s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts
**Same.** Simone gone. Graham has moved into Nia's empty chair. Two cups on the
table, one never touched — Nia never drank hers.

> **GRAHAM:** Dinner. Thursday.
> **CHI:** I'm seeing someone.
> **GRAHAM:** Are you?
> *(beat — she has not defined it, and she knows it)*
> **CHI:** ...We haven't said.
> **GRAHAM:** Then it's Thursday. *(beat)* One thing, so it's said now. I'm
> separated. Eleven months. It's civil.
> **CHI:** Separated is married.
> **GRAHAM:** Separated is separated.

He leaves a card on the table and goes. Chi looks at it. **She picks it up.** Hold
three seconds. Cut to black.

*⚠️ **"Are you?" is the question Chi cannot answer and it is why Graham exists.**
She and Kel have not said. Kel said "next time." Nobody has used a word. **"We
haven't said" is true and it is the first time in the season Chi has conceded a
point to a man in full.***

*⚠️ **"Separated is married." is Chi's own line — she has said it to Nia, in
substance, for years.** She says it to him flat and immediate, the way she says
everything true. And **"Separated is separated"** is him not arguing, just
restating, which is what she does. **Then she picks up the card.** No hesitation
on the pick-up, one beat before it. She has heard herself and done it anyway. That
is the show's engine and it has never once run on Chi until now.*

*The card: **never legible.** A white card, a dark edge, nothing readable. Describe
the absence.*

*He said the two things — kids is in N4, separated is here — **plainly, early, and
without being asked.** That is more than Dorian has managed in three weeks. It is
also, still, a married man asking her to dinner. Both are true. Nobody says either.*
