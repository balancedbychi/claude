# EXCLUSIVE — the new order
## Episode 3: "Two Phones"

**Status: PROPOSED — nothing locked, nothing spent.** Read
`exclusive/new-order-README.md` first.

**Runtime:** 1:32 · 6 clips · 92 seconds
**Format:** One set, one table, one conversation. Daytime. The table format.
**Logline:** Nia did not leave Dorian. She got a second man instead, and she has an
argument for it. Chi tries to steer her away from Simone and steers straight into
her own motive.

---

## WHAT THIS EPISODE IS

**The villain episode, and the villain is Nia — for about forty seconds.**

Then she stops being it, because she is not actually a villain, she is a woman who
has learned exactly the wrong lesson at speed and is defending it brilliantly.
**She is never played as a fool and she is never punished.** She wins the middle of
this episode outright — Clip 3 is the hardest hit anyone lands on Chi all season
and it is completely accurate.

And she is also the person who throws water in her best friend's face in a public
café. Both. **CLAUDE.md §1's "never moralise" is the load-bearing rule here**: the
show does not comment on any of it. No one says "this isn't you." No one says "what
happened to you." The audience supplies every bit of the wince.

### The thing Nia is doing, plainly

She is seeing Dorian and she is seeing a man called Elias, and she is lying to
both, and **she has a whole theory about why it is fine.** That theory is funny,
internally consistent, delivered fast, and wrong, and nobody in the scene refutes
it — Chi just names it in three words and moves on.

### Elias costs nothing and must stay that way

**He is never seen and never heard.** He exists entirely inside one sentence of
Nia's. A grown man with a car and a mother he calls. That is funnier, cheaper and
more damning than any casting decision, and the second he appears on screen the
joke dies. **No reference, no voice, no clip, ever.**

### Where Chi loses it

Chi spends four clips being right and then makes the only unforced error of her
life: **she tells Nia not to call Simone.** It is good advice. It is also entirely
self-interested, Nia can smell it, and everything from that line to the end of the
season is the consequence.

**She is not caught lying in this episode. She is caught caring about the wrong
thing.** That is a much harder note and it is the whole back half.

## THE SCRIPT

Six clips, 92 seconds, one continuous conversation at one small round café table.
`Sucré-Coffee-Shop`, daytime, the seated two-shot for the entire runtime. 50mm
except where noted. Cuts land on the first syllable.

**Two holds are allowed: the three seconds of nothing at the end of C5, and the
last six seconds of C6.**

---

### CLIP 01 — "Also Mine" · 13s · 50mm · f/2.2 · two cuts
**Int. Sucré — day.** Two cups already on the table; Chi got here first and ordered
for her, as always. Nia sits down in a rush, bright, twenty minutes late, and puts
**two phones** on the table, face down, side by side.

She does not hide them. That is the point.

> **CHI:** Whose is that?
> **NIA:** Mine.
> **CHI:** And the other one?
> **NIA:** Also mine.
> **CHI:** Mm.
> **NIA:** Don't.
> **CHI:** I didn't say anything.
> **NIA:** You said "mm."

*⚠️ **The two phones must be established in the first two seconds and must stay in
frame for the entire episode.** Face down, side by side, near Nia's hand. They are
the title and the whole argument. One of them lights up silently, once, in C4 — and
**Nia turns it further face-down without looking at it**, which is the most damning
thing she does all episode and has no line attached.*

*Chi's four lines are nine words. **"Mm." does the work of a paragraph** and Nia
knows exactly what it means, which is the joke — "You said 'mm'" is the closest
thing this show has to a catchphrase and it should be played warm. **These two are
still friends in this clip.** If C1 plays cold, C6 has nothing to break.*

*The second cup is on the table before Nia sits. Chi ordered ahead. **It makes her
warm before she is direct** and it is the reason the episode is not an
interrogation. Do not cut it for time.*

---

### CLIP 02 — "It's a Queue" · 14s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts
**Same.** Nia's aria. Fast, delighted, completely unbothered, and she has clearly
rehearsed some of it.

> **NIA:** His name is Elias. He's a grown man with a car and a mother he calls.
> Dorian has Simone, I have Elias, nobody's the idiot. That's not cheating, Chi.
> That's parity.
> **CHI:** Parity.
> **NIA:** It's maths.
> **CHI:** It's a queue.

*⚠️ **Nia's tell is length and this is the longest run of dialogue she has ever
had.** She is losing catastrophically and she is talking at about 3.8 words a
second to cover it. **Play it fast, bright and genuinely funny** — she is enjoying
the bit, the audience should enjoy the bit, and nobody should feel superior to her
for one frame of it.*

*"a mother he calls" is the specific detail that sells the whole character and it
should get the lightest possible touch — she is listing his qualifications and that
is the best one she has.*

*⚠️ **"It's a queue." is Chi's entire function in one line.** Three words against
thirty-three. No emphasis, no pleasure, no cruelty, and **she does not look up from
her cup when she says it.** She is not scoring a point; she is correcting a figure.
The line is British-inflected for Nia's ear — Chi has picked up one word from
twenty years of her — and it lands like a slap that hasn't happened yet.*

*Do not let the model put a reaction on Nia. **Cut before she responds.** The clip
ends on the word "queue."*

---

### CLIP 03 — "That's Fair" · 16s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts
**Same.** And Nia hits back, and she hits the exact spot.

> **NIA:** You know what your problem is? You've been right about every man for
> twenty years and you've got nothing to show for it but a very clean apartment.
> *(beat)*
> **CHI:** That's fair.
> **NIA:** ...Don't do that.
> **CHI:** You wanted to land one. You landed it.

*⚠️ **This is the cruellest line in the series and it is aimed with total
precision** at the ache Chi is least direct about: forty, wants a child, no partner,
one glass and one candle. Nia knows exactly where it is because she has been let in
there. **She says it fast and she regrets it inside half a second**, and the regret
must not become an apology.*

*⚠️ **Chi agreeing is the whole clip and it is worse than any counter.** "That's
fair" is flat, immediate, and completely sincere — she is not being wounded, she is
not being noble, she is **conceding a point because it is correct**, which is what
she has always done and the first time it has cost her anything.*

*"Don't do that" is Nia asking her to please fight back, because a Chi who takes it
makes Nia the villain, and Nia has just worked that out.*

*⚠️ **"That's fair" is a deliberate quotation.** It is the phrase Nia used about
Dorian in the prologue — the thing she said to him when he would not commit, and
repeated to Chi as though it were her own idea. **Chi is handing it back.** She is
almost certainly not doing it consciously and the show never points at it. The
alternate title of that prologue episode was "That's Fair" for exactly this reason;
if the two are ever cut together, this is the hinge.*

*"You wanted to land one. You landed it." is scoring the exchange, not conceding
the war. It is generous and it is unbearable.*

### CLIP 04 — "Don't Call Her" · 13s · 50mm · f/2.2 · two cuts
**Same.** Chi makes her mistake. It takes eleven words.

> **CHI:** Don't call Simone.
> **NIA:** Why would I call Simone?
> **CHI:** Because you already have her number.
> *(Nia says nothing. Caught. **One of the two phones lights up, face down. She
> turns it further over without looking at it.**)*
> **CHI:** Don't call her, Nia.
> **NIA:** Why do you care?

*⚠️ **"Why do you care?" is the question the rest of the season answers** and it
must land as a genuine question, not an accusation. Nia is not suspicious yet. She
is irritated, and she has noticed something is the wrong shape, and she will not
work out what until Simone tells her in N4.*

*⚠️ **Chi uses Nia's name in the fifth line** and that is the tell. She has said
"Nia." as a full sentence for seven episodes to end an argument; **here it is not
ending anything, it is pushing**, and pushing is not something Chi does. It is the
same borrowing we saw her do with Kel in N2 C6 — **under pressure Chi loses her own
voice and starts using other people's moves.** Same tell, second occurrence,
nobody remarks on it either time.*

*The phone lighting up is silent, brief, and unremarked. **No notification sound,
no visible screen content, no name, no push-in on it.** Just the glow on the table
and her hand turning it over. Anything more is a different, worse scene.*

### CLIP 05 — "At That Party" · 18s · 50mm → 75mm · f/2.0 · three cuts
**Same.** Push to 75mm across the clip, almost imperceptible.

> **NIA:** She knew your name.
> **CHI:** We've met.
> **NIA:** You said "here." At the party. You said you met her *here*.
> **CHI:** I did.
> **NIA:** At a party you left at midnight.
> *(Chi says nothing.)*
> **NIA:** Chi. Look at me and tell me you met that woman at that party.

*(Chi looks at her. **Chi says nothing.** Three full seconds. The nothing is the
answer and both of them know it.)*

*⚠️ **The three seconds must be declared in the prompt or they get filled:** "this
clip is the exception, the silence after Nia's last line is the point, do not fill
it, Chi does not speak, no ambience swell, no music, no third voice, no café voice
audible."*

*⚠️ **Chi does the honourable thing here and it is the worst possible move.** Asked
directly, she will not lie again — she lied in N1 C6 and to Kel in N2 C6 and she
has reached the end of what she can do. **So she says nothing, which is not a
refusal to lie, it is a confession she has not authorised.** Play it as someone
choosing silence over a third lie and understanding, in real time, what the silence
costs.*

*No guilt face. No apology face. **She holds Nia's eyes for the whole three
seconds** — that is the only thing that keeps her from looking small, and it is also
what makes the water inevitable.*

*Nia's "here" gets a light stress as a quotation — she is quoting Chi's own word
back at her, exactly as Chi quoted "eleven" back at Nia in the prologue. **Third
instance of the season's quotation habit** and the first time it is used as a
weapon rather than a correction.*

---

### CLIP 06 — "The Actual Question" · 18s · 50mm · f/2.2 · three cuts
**Same.** And then Nia throws the water.

**Shoot the two ends, never the middle.** Nia's hand closes on the water glass. The
arm starts up. **CUT ON THE UPSWING.** Sound carries it.

**CUT TO: Chi, already wet.** Water running off her jaw. One strand of hair stuck
to her cheek. A dark patch spreading across the shoulder of her top. A puddle on
the table, spreading toward the two phones. **She has not moved. She does not wipe
her face. She does not blink it away. She does not look away from Nia.**

> **CHI:** Ask me the actual question.

*(Nia cannot. She takes both phones off the table and goes.)*

Chi alone at the table, wet, in a café where every single person is now looking at
her and pretending not to. She picks up a paper napkin. She wipes her face — slowly,
without hurrying, without embarrassment. She folds the napkin. She sets it down.
She is still sitting there when we cut to black.

*⚠️ **NEVER RENDER THE WATER IN FLIGHT.** Liquid mid-air is the one thing in this
sequence with no chance of coming back clean, and a two-element contact frame is
where faces break. The wet face is a **state** — still, held, sustained — which is
exactly what the model is good at and exactly what Chi does anyway. Cut on the
upswing, land on the aftermath, let the sound do the middle. **Do not ask for a
splash.** Ask for the state, item by item: water off the jaw, the strand of hair,
the dark patch on the shoulder, the puddle, absolute stillness.*

*⚠️ **"Ask me the actual question." is the bravest thing Chi does all season.** She
is inviting it. She has just been assaulted in public and her response is to open
the door — and **Nia cannot walk through it**, which is why there is a fourth
episode. Delivered level, unhurried, not wounded, not defiant. It is an offer.*

*⚠️ **Chi taking it without moving is the first half of a rhyme.** The slap in N4
lands the same way — she does not move — and **the difference is what she does
next**: here she has one line and then sits in it; there she has the longest speech
of her life. If both are played identically the rhyme is worth nothing. **Here she
is composed. There she is not.***

*The napkin is the ending. **Do not have her cry, do not have her shake, do not
have her look around the café.** She wipes her face the way you would after soup.
The composure is the tragedy — she has had a lot of practice being fine in public.*

*Everyone in the café is looking and **nobody intervenes, nobody approaches, no
waiter, no reaction shot of a stranger.** Background only, in soft focus, bodies
turned very slightly toward her. If a bystander gets a close-up the scene becomes
about them.*

## CONTINUITY

- **Nia's neckline never changes between clips** — never raised, never lowered,
  never swapped. Choker only if the reference leaves her throat bare, **never on
  top of fabric**. Gold ring, same hand, same finger, every clip.
- **Chi wears NO RING, ever** — named object by object, nothing on the fourth
  finger of her left hand. Gold watch only. Nails deep red. ⚠️ **This is a plot
  fact this season, not just continuity.**
- **Chi's hair sweeps to HER OWN RIGHT**, deep side part on the LEFT of her head,
  falling down her right side — **facing camera, screen LEFT.** ⚠️ In C6 it is wet
  on one side and the sweep still holds; **one strand stuck to her cheek is
  specified, the style is not otherwise disturbed.**
- **Clothes adapt to Chi's body, never the reverse** — tailored realistically to her
  real full-figured proportions.
- **Seated two-shot at a small round café table for the entire runtime.** Boots,
  heels and bags sit below frame and are pinned for completeness only.
- **Exactly two cups, identical**, one per woman — every wrong alternative named.
  Levels are a **fixed constant**, not a clock; the differential-level idea failed
  to render in the prologue and is not retried.
- **Daylight, warm, natural.** Never night, never blue-dominant, never coloured.
- **Background: soft, sparse, nobody at the adjacent tables in focus, nobody
  looking at the lens.** Until C6, when everyone is looking and still nobody is in
  focus.
- **No music in the café.** Room tone only.
