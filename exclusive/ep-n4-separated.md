# EXCLUSIVE — the new order
## Episode 4: "Separated"

**Status: PROPOSED — nothing locked, nothing spent.** Written 16 Sep 2026. Read
`exclusive/new-order-README.md` first.

**Runtime:** 1:43 · 6 clips · 103 seconds
**Format:** A phone call, a kitchen, a loft, and then Chi's flat for the rest.
**Logline:** Kel finds out about the lawyer from Dorian. Nia makes Dorian say the
name and slaps him on it. Nia walks in on the lawyer on Chi's sofa, and the season
ends with two glasses, one sleeping friend, and a foil-wrapped plate on the mat.

---

## THE TITLE

Graham's word. What Nia and Dorian become. And the two women at the end — separate
stories, one sofa. **Nobody says the title as a line after C1.**

---

## WHAT HAS TO HAPPEN AND WHAT MUST NOT

**Must happen:**
- Nia is the one with the advice, and it is correct, and Chi does not take it.
- Chi says her ache out loud, plainly, to the wrong man: *"He says he wants kids."*
- Nia asks Dorian straight, gets a straight answer, and hits him for it.
- The two women end the season on one sofa. **Not a rift. Never a rift.**

**Must not:**
- **Nobody moralises.** Not about the slap, not about Graham, not about Elias. No
  "you brought this on yourself," no "this isn't you." The audience supplies the
  wince.
- **Dorian is not a villain.** "I never lied to you" is true. That is the problem.
- **Kel is not bitter.** He is hurt in a way he does not name, and he lets her go.
- **Graham is not exposed.** He said separated. He said kids. He leaves gracefully
  when Nia arrives. He is a married man on a sofa and a decent one, and both are
  true.
- **Chi does not choose.** The season ends with the plate in one hand and the phone
  ringing in the other.
- **No music sting on the slap.**

### CLIP 01 — "You Taught Me That" · 16s · 50mm · f/2.0 · intercut
**Int. Nia's bedroom — night / Int. Chi's living room — night.** Nia on the bed,
phone. Chi on the sofa — **two glasses on the marble table, one candle.** The second
glass has not moved since N2.

> **NIA:** Separated is MARRIED, Chi. You taught me that.
> **CHI:** It's dinner.
> **NIA:** It's Thursday.
> **CHI:** Nia.
> **NIA:** Does Kel know?
> *(Chi says nothing.)*
> **NIA:** Chi.
> **CHI:** I'll tell him.
> **NIA:** When?
> **CHI** *(beat)*: After.
> **NIA:** After WHAT?

Chi hangs up. Stay with Nia. She looks at the phone. **Chi has never hung up on
her mid-question.** Hold two seconds.

*⚠️ **The inversion is complete and it is the freshest thing in the season.** Nia
has the accurate sentence and Chi has "It's dinner," which is Nia's kind of answer.
**Nia's delivery is not triumphant.** She is worried. She is doing what Chi does,
and it does not feel like winning.*

*"It's Thursday" is Nia quoting Graham's line from N3 C6 — she has it from Chi,
which means Chi told her, which means Chi wanted to be talked out of it and is not
letting herself be. **Light stress, as a quotation.***

*Chi's "Nia." is the sound that has ended arguments for seven episodes. **Here it
ends nothing.** Nia talks straight through it. That is the first time.*

*"After." / "After WHAT?" — the hang-up on Nia's question is Chi's only cruelty of
the season and she does not mean it as one. She has run out of true things to say.*

### CLIP 02 — "I Was Going to Say a Lawyer" · 20s · 50mm · f/2.2 · two cuts
**Int. Dorian's kitchen — night.** Kel at the sink, back to the door, sleeves up.
Chi in the doorway. **He does not turn around.**

> **KEL:** Dorian saw you Thursday.
> **CHI** *(stops)*: ...It was dinner.
> **KEL:** With a lawyer.
> *(beat)*
> **KEL:** Is it because he's—
> **CHI:** Don't.
> **KEL:** I was going to say a lawyer.
> *(beat)*
> **CHI:** ...He says he wants kids.

Kel turns off the tap. Turns around. Looks at her for a long moment.

> **KEL:** I'm fifty-three, Chi.
> **CHI:** I know.
> **KEL:** ...Okay.

He turns back to the sink. She stands there. **She has never been the one standing
there.** Hold on her back, then cut.

*⚠️ **"He says he wants kids" is the season.** The embarrassing thing, said plainly,
by the woman who is least direct about it, to the wrong man, as an explanation.
**No hesitation on it, no tears, no looking away.** She says it like a fact because
it is one. **Kel hears it as the answer to "what do you want, Chi" from N2 C3** —
she has finally told him, and it is a reason to leave.*

*⚠️ **"Is it because he's—" / "Don't." / "I was going to say a lawyer."** — the
line both audiences are waiting for, defused and named at the same time. Kel is
not asking about race. Chi assumes he is. **He lets her assumption sit for exactly
one beat and then corrects it, gently, and the correction is the joke.** No edge
from him, no relief from her. Play it as two people who both know what the other
was about to think.*

*"I'm fifty-three, Chi" is the same line as N2, **and this time it is an answer.** He
is not going to say he wants a child at fifty-three to keep her. He is too honest,
and that is what she liked. **"...Okay." is withdrawal, not anger.** He goes back to
the dishes and he does not look at her again.*

*Dorian told him. Dorian, who is doing this to Nia. **Nobody points at the
hypocrisy** — Chi does not say "Dorian, who—" and Kel does not defend him. The
audience has the whole picture and the scene does not need it.*

### CLIP 03 — "Say Her Name" · 19s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts · **THE SLAP**
**Int. Dorian's loft, window end — night.** No party. The room empty, one lamp,
the city behind the glass. Dorian with a tumbler. Nia has come to ask.

> **NIA:** Was it ever just me?
> **DORIAN:** It was always you.
> **NIA:** That's not what I asked.
> **DORIAN** *(beat)*: ...No.
> **NIA:** Say her name.
> **DORIAN:** Simone.

**SLAP — on "Simone."** Nia's hand up, at the top of its arc. **CUT ON THE WINDUP.**
The sound lands over the cut.

**Aftermath.** Dorian's head turned. One cheek darker. He does not touch it.

> **DORIAN:** I never lied to you.
> **NIA:** No. You just never said.

She goes. He stands there with the whiskey. He is not a villain. He is a man who
never said. Hold two seconds on him alone.

*⚠️ **NEVER RENDER THE CONTACT.** Two character elements touching is where faces
break and an impact frame is the worst instance of it. Cut at the top of the arm's
arc. Open the aftermath on his head already turned. **Name and negate: hands
meeting, contact, impact, a face deforming, a hand across a cheek, two figures
overlapping, motion blur across a face.***

*⚠️ **She makes him say it and then hits him for saying it.** That is messy and
real and the show does not resolve it. **"It was always you" is true. "No" is true.
Both at once is Dorian.***

*⚠️ **"You just never said."** — Nia has Chi's diagnosis line now. She has learned
the thing. It is the most accurate sentence she has ever spoken and she walks out on
it. **Flat, not shouted, not crying.** The crying is in C4.*

*Dorian not touching his cheek is the same gesture as everyone who has been hit
this season — the wine, the water, now this. **The person who takes it does not
move.***

### CLIP 04 — "Is This the Lawyer" · 19s · 50mm · f/2.0 · three cuts
**Int. Chi's living room — night.** Graham on the sofa. Chi in her corner, legs
toward him. **Two glasses on the marble table** — his and hers.

> **GRAHAM:** I want this. I'm too old to pretend I don't.
> **CHI:** You're married.
> **GRAHAM:** Separated.
> **CHI:** Mm.

A knock. Chi opens the door. **Nia.** Mascara. The dress from the loft.

> **NIA** *(sees Graham)*: Is this the lawyer?
> **GRAHAM** *(standing)*: Graham.
> **NIA:** Nia. I just hit a man.
> **GRAHAM** *(to Chi, quietly, kindly)*: I'll call you.

He goes. Nia watches him go.

> **NIA:** He's tall.
> **CHI:** Sit down.

*⚠️ **Graham is good here and it matters.** "I want this" is direct and warm and he
means it. Chi's "Mm." is not a no. **She is deciding, on the sofa, and the knock
interrupts the decision.** If
the audience does not believe she might have said yes, C6 has no weight.*

*"I just hit a man." — Nia announcing it flat to a stranger in a doorway. **Comedy
inside tragedy, the show's register.** Graham's "I'll call you" is graceful —
quiet, to Chi, no pressure — and he leaves without a beat of awkwardness. **He is
not exposed and not embarrassed. He is a man who knows when to leave a room.***

*"He's tall." / "Sit down." — Nia's first line after watching him go is an
assessment, because she cannot help it, and Chi's is an instruction, because she
cannot either.*

### CLIP 05 — "That's What I Said" · 19s · 50mm · f/2.0 · two cuts
**Same.** Nia on the sofa. She picks up **Graham's glass** and drinks from it. Chi
lets her.

> **CHI:** He's separated.
> **NIA:** You said that's married.
> **CHI:** It is.
> **NIA:** So why's he on your sofa.
> **CHI** *(beat)*: He said the thing I wanted to hear.
> **NIA:** ...Yeah.

She leans into Chi. Chi lets her. Two glasses on the table.

> **NIA:** What did Kel say?
> **CHI** *(beat)*: "I'm fifty-three."
> **NIA:** That's not an answer.
> **CHI:** That's what I said.

*⚠️ **"He said the thing I wanted to hear" is the most honest line Chi has in the
season and she gets it in eight words.** Nia's "...Yeah." is her knowing —
she did the same thing with Dorian for three weeks — and it is the warmest sound she
makes all episode.*

*⚠️ **"That's not an answer." / "That's what I said."** — the season's thesis. Nia
says to Chi exactly what Chi said to Kel in N2 C3, about the same non-answer,
without knowing it. **They are the same person ten years apart.** Chi's line is
almost a laugh. It is the first time she has found any of this funny.*

*Nia drinking from Graham's glass is the only thing she does with it — **no line
about it.** The glass has been his, and now it is hers, and the count on the table
is still two.*

*⚠️ **This is the friendship and it is not in question.** Nia leaning on her, Chi
letting her. **No rift, no distance, no "we need to talk."** Whatever the men do,
this is the thing that holds.*

### CLIP 06 — "The Mat" · 10s · 50mm · f/2.2 · locked static · **NO DIALOGUE**
**Same.** Later. Nia asleep on the sofa under a throw. Two glasses on the table.
The candle low.

Chi's phone lights up on the marble. She looks at it. **She turns it face down.**

She goes to the door. Opens it. **A foil-wrapped plate on the doormat.** Handwriting
on the foil.

She looks at it. She bends and picks it up. **In her other hand the phone starts to
ring, face down, buzzing against her palm.** She stands in the doorway with the
plate in one hand and the ringing phone in the other. Hold. Cut to black.

*⚠️ **Nothing on the phone screen is ever legible.** A glow, a banner shape, nothing
readable — "describe the absence." The audience does not need to read it: **a phone
she turns face down is Graham, a plate on the mat is Kel**, and the foil with
handwriting is N1 C6's foil. Same foil, same marker, same man.*

*⚠️ **She picks up the plate and the phone rings.** Both in her hands. **She does
not choose and the episode does not choose for her.** Do not let a note add a beat
that tells us which — no look at the phone, no look at the plate, no step in either
direction. The doorway, both hands full, hold, black.*

*⚠️ **Declare the silence:** "this clip is the exception, there is no dialogue, the
silence is the point, do not fill it, no music, no score, no voice, no third person."
The phone buzz is the only sound in the last four seconds. **ffmpeg duck on the
tail; do not spend a take fighting the swell.***

*Bookend of the series' first shot: *one glass, one candle, nobody.* **Now: two
glasses, one candle, one sleeping friend, and a man who left a plate.***
