## WHAT THE EPISODE IS ABOUT — THE USER'S BRIEF, VERBATIM IN SUBSTANCE

> Nia meets Chi at Sucré to tell her that her and Dorian are still going strong.
> Chi's question is, is he still dating around — have you guys become exclusive?
> And Nia doesn't want to admit that they aren't fully exclusive yet, though she
> knows that she's tried to have the conversations with Dorian. He hasn't
> committed to her fully yet. So they're still dating. But she's hopeful that he
> will make her his girlfriend.

Four things in that brief are load-bearing and the script must not soften any of
them:

1. **Nia arrives claiming a win.** She is not asking for help. She is reporting
   good news, and Chi's question is what turns the scene.
2. **She has tried MORE THAN ONCE.** "The conversations", plural. That is the
   difference between a woman who has not asked and a woman who has asked and been
   answered in a way she agreed to. The second is the show.
3. **They are still dating.** Not a misunderstanding, not a grey area she has not
   checked. She knows.
4. **She is hopeful.** The episode ends with her hope intact and unpunished.
   Section 1: never moralise, the audience supplies the wince.

**This is the episode where the series title gets asked as a question out loud.**
"Are you exclusive?" is Chi's line and it lands at the end of Clip 2.

### Why the title is still "Available"

"Exclusive" collides with the series name in any listing. **"Available" is Chi's
verdict and the exact inverse of the question Nia cannot answer** — Nia hears the
word as *single*, Chi means *still shopping*, and neither of them explains it.
Alternative if you want the wince in the title instead: **"That's Fair"**, which is
what Nia says to him and repeats to Chi in Clip 3.

## WHY THIS SHAPE

Episode 1 was the phone. Episode 2 was the party — five sets, four characters,
roughly 1,420 credits. Section 1 names two formats for this show, **on the phone or
across a table**, and the table has not been used. It is also the cheapest episode
the series can make: the set is already locked, both faces are locked, both voices
are locked, and **no new speaking character appears at all.**

### What it inherits from "The Caterer"

- Dorian called Nia **"my friend"** in front of the group. Neither woman has said
  it out loud since, and neither says it here. It is the reason Chi asks.
- Chi left at midnight exactly as she said she would. Nia stayed.
- Chi had been counting the days — **"Twelve."** — and Nia caught her at it.

### The counting motif pays off

Ep 1: eleven days. Ep 2: Chi corrects it to twelve and Nia mocks her for counting.
**Ep 3: Nia volunteers "twenty-one days" unprompted**, which proves she has been
counting too. Chi does not point that out. She drinks. One line, no comment.

### Chi's blind spot for this arc

Section 1 requires one per arc. Cornered in Clip 5, Nia asks Chi when she last got
anything wrong about a man, and Chi does not defend it — she agrees. Her admission
is the most honest line she has had in three episodes and it reframes her: she
thinks being right has protected her, and it has also cost her.

### Chi ordered Nia's coffee before she arrived

The user's detail, and it does more work than a line of dialogue. Chi got there
first and ordered for her, so the second cup is already on the table when Nia sits
down and Chi slides it across without a word. **It makes Chi warm before she is
direct** — she is not prosecuting Nia, she is feeding her — and section 1's
"comforting but direct" needs that or Clip 2 reads as an interrogation.

It also sets the episode's clock. Chi arrived first, so **her cup starts lower than
Nia's**, and both go down and neither is ever refilled.

## THE RULES THIS EPISODE ADDS

- **Six clips of two women alternating lines at a table is the highest
  attribution-risk shape this series has.** It is the shape that cost Episode 2
  Clip 1 five takes. The proven answer already exists and this episode is built on
  it: **the locked two-shot from Clip 5** — both faces in frame at the same size,
  both lit evenly from the front, three-quarter and turned INWARD toward each
  other, no cuts. Never parallel to the lens; that was the Clip 5 eyeline failure.
- **Seating is a series-level lock: NIA SCREEN LEFT, CHI SCREEN RIGHT**, every
  clip, no reverses. Same arrangement as Episode 2 Clip 5, so it carries across
  episodes.
- **A cut must be earned.** The default is one unbroken take per clip. Section 4
  says alternating speakers need cuts; Episode 2 proved in practice that the locked
  two-shot beats cutting for attribution, and the user approved it. Any cut is a
  per-clip decision with a reason, not a default.
- **The coffee is the clock.** One continuous conversation, so nothing changes
  except the cups going down. **Chi's starts lower than Nia's** because she arrived
  first. Levels drop monotonically and **never refill.**
- **Exactly TWO cups exist**, both the same, one in front of each woman, and no
  third cup, spare, saucer, glass or empty on any surface in any frame. Quantities
  are absolute or they drift.
- **Nia's phone is face-UP on the marble from the first frame of Clip 1.** It has
  to already be there, or the buzz in Clip 6 is an invented object. One-directional:
  once it goes face-down it stays face-down.
- **CAFÉ POPULATION — decided: TWO OR THREE patrons, never more, in any frame.**
  Seated, deep, soft, at the edges. They never cross behind the leads' heads, never
  look at the lens, and **not one word from any of them is ever intelligible** — a
  low indistinct murmur under the scene. This is Episode 2's crowd rule rescaled;
  "a coffee shop" left unstated will populate itself differently in every clip.
- **ChiChi is honey-blonde.** Her pinned identity block says so and it is
  byte-frozen. `ChiChi-Series-Look` (`bc1bd310`) describes dark brown hair, is
  retired, and must not be picked up or used to "correct" the pinned block.
- **Chi wears NO RING**, named object by object, with her gold watch the single
  permitted exception. It appeared anyway in Episode 2 despite a generic negation.
- **Chi's hair sweeps to HER OWN RIGHT**, which reads as SCREEN LEFT facing camera.
  State it both ways, every prompt.
- **Dorian is quoted, never impersonated.** Nia reports three of his lines in Clip 3
  in her own voice, flat, the way you repeat something you have memorised. She does
  not do an impression and the model must not render a male voice anywhere in this
  episode.
- **Nobody moralises and nobody wins.** Chi gets the accurate line, Nia gets the
  last action, and the episode ends with her hope intact.
- **Word balance is restored.** Episode 2 Clip 5 inverted section 1's rule as a
  declared exception, with a warning that a second inversion would be drift. Across
  this episode: **Nia 193 words, Chi 77.**

## THE SCRIPT

Six clips, 126 seconds, one continuous conversation at one table. Daytime.
Panavision Millennium XL2, Primo lenses, eye-level at seated height.

### Clip 01 — "Going strong" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 15s

**Int. Sucré, window table — day.** Chi is already there, her coffee already
partway down, and **a second cup sits waiting on Nia's side of the table.** The
camera is already on the locked two-shot when **Nia arrives into the frame** and
sits down hard.

> NIA: "Don't say anything yet."
> CHICHI: "I haven't."
> NIA: "You're doing the face."
> CHICHI: "This is my face."
> *(Chi slides the second cup across to her. No line.)*
> NIA: "We're good. We're actually good. Twenty-one days and we're still good."
> CHICHI: "Mm."

*Chi drinks on the number — a completed, unhurried movement, the exact inverse of
the interrupted one in Episode 2 Clip 4. It means: I am not going to fight you about
this. She says nothing about the number and the audience does the arithmetic.*

**Nia arrives into the FRAME, not into the room.** She does not walk in from the
door and cross the café. Nine seconds of tracking one woman is what made ChiChi the
subject of Episode 2 Clip 1 and sent three of its four lines to her mouth. Chi is in
shot from frame one, so the two-shot is the subject from frame one.

**The slid cup is the warmth the rest of the episode spends.** Chi asks a hard
question ninety seconds from now; she is allowed to, because she ordered first.

### Clip 02 — "Are you exclusive?" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 20s

> CHICHI: "Is he still dating other people?"
> NIA: "What kind of question is that?"
> CHICHI: "The kind with an answer."
> NIA: "We're not— it's not like that. We see each other three, four times a week now, he calls me, he actually calls, nobody calls anymore, Chi."
> CHICHI: "Nia."
> NIA: "What."
> CHICHI: "Are you exclusive?"

*The clip ends ON the question, unanswered, and holds. Chi asks it twice in twenty
seconds and the second version is three words, because the first one got
twenty-six words back and that is its own answer. Length is Nia's tell and this is
the clip that proves it.*

⚠️ **"Nia." / "What." are adjacent one-word lines and that is the single most
dangerous pair in the episode.** Section 7: lines under about a second are too short
for the discriminator to separate, and adjacent short lines are where attribution
slips. Name both wrong answers in the prompt.

### Clip 03 — "That's fair" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 22s

**The admission, and the reason the episode exists.**

> NIA: "I've asked him."
> CHICHI: "When?"
> NIA: "...A few times."
> *(Chi says nothing. She waits. The silence is declared and it belongs to her.)*
> NIA: "The first time he said he doesn't like labels. The second time he said we're figuring it out. And last week he said he doesn't want to rush something that's working."
> CHICHI: "And what did you say?"
> NIA: "I said that's fair."

*Three non-answers, each more reasonable-sounding than the last, and none of them
villainous — that is section 1's "no villains" doing its job. He is not lying to
her and he never has been.*

***"I said that's fair" is the most embarrassing line in the series so far and it
must be played completely straight.*** Not sheepish, not a confession, not a woman
who knows she is being laughed at. **Nia still half-believes it was the right
move** — that is what makes it recognition rather than humiliation. The audience
winces; Nia does not.

**Nia recites his three lines flat**, the way you repeat something you have gone
over on your own many times. No impression, no male voice, no mimicry.

### Clip 04 — "Available" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 27s · ENDS ON A HOLD

> NIA: "Just say it."
> CHICHI: "You don't want me to."
> NIA: "I'm asking you to say it. You've had the face on since I sat down, so say it — I'd rather hear it than look at it."
> CHICHI: "You want me to say it so you can decide I'm wrong."
> NIA: "Chi."
> *(beat — Chi sets her cup down. One deliberate movement.)*
> CHICHI: "He's not lying to you. He's told you three times. He's available."
> NIA: "That's not— being available isn't a bad thing."
> CHICHI: "To who?"

***This clip is the episode.*** *It turns on one word meaning two things. Nia hears
"available" as single; Chi means still shopping. Neither of them explains it and the
prompt must not have anyone explain it. "To who?" ends the exchange in two words and
the clip holds on it — no reply, no reaction shot, no music swell.*

**"He's told you three times" is a callback to Clip 3 and it is what makes Chi's
read land as observation rather than opinion.** She is not guessing at him. She is
repeating back what Nia just told her.

**Chi sets her cup down before the verdict** — she is still, so her one deliberate
movement in twenty-seven seconds is the loudest thing she does, and it lands right
before the most useful sentence she says. Same mechanic as the glass on the rail in
Episode 2 Clip 5. **The cup stays where she set it for the rest of the episode.**

⚠️ **Longest clip at 27s and the most dangerous.** Eight lines alternating between
two women is the Episode 2 Clip 1 shape. The staging is Clip 5's because that is
what resolved it. **Do not add a cut here to "help"** — that was tried three times
and never once worked.

### Clip 05 — "By now" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 25s

> NIA: "When did you last get it wrong?"
> CHICHI: "What?"
> NIA: "About a man. When did you last get it wrong about a man?"
> *(Chi says nothing. Declared silence, hers.)*
> NIA: "Because I'd rather get it wrong. I'd rather get it wrong twice a year than sit in a beautiful flat alone being right."
> *(beat — that went further than Nia meant it to)*
> NIA: "...Chi—"
> CHICHI: "No. You're right."
> *(beat)*
> CHICHI: "I'd like to have gotten it wrong with someone by now."

*Nia's one accurate hit in three episodes, and it is not cruel — it is what comes
out when she is cornered, and she hears it land and tries to take it back. Chi does
not retaliate and does not perform being hurt. She agrees. "A beautiful flat being
right" is Episode 1's empty sectional, named out loud.*

**This is the blind spot.** Chi envies Nia's ability to get it wrong. Played flat
and factual — never wistful, never tearful, never a confession, no wet eyes. She
says it the way she says everything else and then it is over.

**"Alone" is the word that makes the scene work.** Without it the line is pointed
but survivable and Nia's "...Chi—" is decoration. With it she has named the thing
Chi is least direct about — section 1's ache, said out loud by the one person
allowed to say it — and the retreat becomes necessary rather than polite. It also
makes **"No. You're right."** enormous, because what Chi is agreeing with is the
word "alone".

### Clip 06 — "He's going to come" · 50mm · f/2.2 · slow push · 17s

> NIA: "He's going to come."
> *(Chi says nothing.)*
> NIA: "He will come, Chi. I know that you're just waiting for the right guy."
> *(Chi does not answer that either. The beat sits.)*
> *(The phone buzzes, face-up on the marble. Both of them see it. Nia turns it face-down without looking at it.)*
> CHICHI: "You can look."
> NIA: "I know."
> *(Beat. She doesn't. Beat. **Then she looks.** Cut to black.)*

*Nia's hope is stated plainly and the show does not punish her for it — that is the
whole of section 1's "never moralise". Then she hands Chi the SAME hope back.
"You're just waiting for the right guy" is the exact optimism Chi has spent the
entire episode refusing to grant Nia, offered now as kindness — the engine of the
series running backwards for four seconds. It is also a repair: Nia said "alone" in
Clip 5, watched it land, and this is her softening it without apologising twice.*

***Chi has no answer to it, and that is the point.*** *She cannot correct it without
being cruel to someone who is trying to be kind to her, and she has just said out
loud that she no longer believes it. So she says nothing — and then she says "You
can look," which is not a change of subject. **It is what she says INSTEAD of
answering.** Chi ducking a question is new, it belongs to this episode, and it is
the first time in three of them that she has not had a reply.*

*Section 1: end one beat earlier than feels comfortable. We never find out what the
message says and we never see Chi react to Nia looking.*

**Ends on NIA, not on Chi.** A reaction shot of Chi would tell the audience what to
think, which is the comment-versus-reaction line in section 3 one level up. The push
leaves the two-shot only AFTER the last word is spoken, exactly as Episode 2 Clip 5
did it — there is no attribution risk in a passage with no dialogue in it.

⚠️ **"come" lands twice and "I know" lands twice. Both are deliberate.** Same ruling
as Episode 2's repeated "eleven days": the repetition is the writing, not a flaw,
and no line gets reworded. Both "come" lines are Nia's, so that pair carries no
cross-speaker risk — but **"I know that you're just waiting for the right guy" (NIA)
and "I know." (NIA) sit either side of "You can look." (CHI)**, and short lines
across a speaker change are exactly where attribution slipped in Episode 2 Clip 1.
Name the wrong answers in the prompt: never give ChiChi either "I know" line.

**"He's going to come" means come AROUND, not arrive**, and the prompt carries that
as a delivery note rather than a rewording. Nia is not expecting him to walk into
Sucré. It reads correctly in context and the echo in "He will come, Chi" makes it
her phrase rather than a slip — but the words alone are slightly open, so the
reading has to come from the delivery.
