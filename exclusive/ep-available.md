**Runtime:** 122 seconds · 6 clips

**Logline:** Twenty-one days in, Nia has finally had the conversation with Dorian.

## WHY THIS SHAPE

Episode 1 was the phone call. Episode 2 was the party — five sets, four
characters, roughly 1,420 credits. Section 1 names two formats for this show:
**on the phone or across a table.** The table has not been used yet, and it is
also the cheapest episode the series can make: one set that is already locked,
two characters that are already locked, two voices that are already locked, and
no new speaking character at all.

The only thing this episode needs from the user is **two wardrobe references.**

### What it inherits from "The Caterer"

- Dorian called Nia **"my friend"** in front of the group. Nia absorbed it. Chi saw
  it happen. Neither has said it out loud since.
- Chi left at midnight exactly as she said she would. Nia stayed.
- Chi had been counting the days — **"Twelve."** — and Nia caught her at it.

### The counting motif pays off here

Ep 1: eleven days. Ep 2: Chi corrects it to twelve, and Nia mocks her for
counting. **Ep 3: Nia volunteers "twenty-one days" unprompted**, which proves she
has been counting too. Chi does not point that out. She takes a drink instead.
That is the whole payoff and it costs one line.

### Chi's blind spot for this arc

Section 1 requires one per arc: she reads other people's lives clearly and her own
poorly. Here Nia lands an accurate hit — *when did you last get it wrong?* — and
Chi does not defend it. Her admission in Clip 5 is the most honest line she has
had in three episodes, and it reframes her: she thinks being right has protected
her, and it has also cost her.

## THE RULES THIS EPISODE ADDS

- **Six clips of two women alternating lines at a table is the highest
  attribution-risk shape this series has.** It is the exact shape that cost
  Episode 2 Clip 1 five takes. The proven answer already exists and this episode
  is built on it: **the locked two-shot from Clip 5** — both faces in frame at the
  same size, both lit evenly from the front, three-quarter and turned INWARD
  toward each other, no cuts. Never parallel to the lens (that is the Clip 5
  eyeline failure), never one face larger than the other.
- **Seating is a series-level lock: NIA SCREEN LEFT, CHI SCREEN RIGHT**, every
  clip, no reverses. Same arrangement as Episode 2 Clip 5, so it carries across
  episodes.
- **A cut must be earned.** The default is one unbroken take per clip. Section 4
  says alternating speakers need cuts; Episode 2 proved in practice that the
  locked two-shot beats cutting for attribution, and the user approved it. Any cut
  is a per-clip decision with a reason, not a default.
- **The coffee is the clock.** One continuous conversation, so nothing changes
  except the cups going down. Levels drop monotonically across the six clips and
  **never refill** — the cheapest continuity available and the thing that makes
  six clips read as one sitting.
- **Exactly TWO cups exist**, one in front of each woman, and no third cup, spare,
  glass or empty on any surface in any frame. Quantities are absolute or they
  drift.
- **Nia's phone is face-UP on the marble from the first frame of Clip 1.** It has
  to already be there, or the buzz in Clip 6 is an invented object. One-directional:
  once it goes face-down it stays face-down.
- **ChiChi is honey-blonde.** Her pinned identity block says so and it is
  byte-frozen. `ChiChi-Series-Look` (`bc1bd310`) describes dark brown hair and is
  retired — do not pick it up and do not "correct" the pinned block toward it.
- **Chi wears NO RING**, named object by object, and her gold watch is the single
  permitted exception. It appeared anyway in Episode 2 despite a generic negation.
- **Chi's hair sweeps to HER OWN RIGHT**, which reads as SCREEN LEFT facing camera.
  State it both ways, every prompt.
- **Nobody moralises and nobody wins.** Chi gets the accurate line and Nia gets the
  last action. The episode does not punish her for it.
- **Word balance is restored.** Episode 2 Clip 5 inverted section 1's rule — Chi 32,
  Nia 12 — as a declared exception, with a warning that a second inversion would be
  drift. Across this episode: **Nia 205 words, Chi 71.** Chi's power is economy
  again, and she ends the biggest exchange in the episode with two words.

## THE SCRIPT

Six clips, 122 seconds, one continuous conversation at one table. Daytime.
Panavision Millennium XL2, Primo lenses, eye-level, seated height.

### Clip 01 — "Twenty-one days" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 16s

**Int. Sucré, window table — day.** Chi is already there, coffee in front of her,
unhurried. The camera is already on the locked two-shot when **Nia arrives into
the frame** and sits down hard.

> NIA: "Don't say anything yet."
> CHICHI: "I haven't."
> NIA: "You're doing the face."
> CHICHI: "This is my face."
> NIA: "We talked. Properly. Sat down and talked, like adults."
> CHICHI: "Mm."
> NIA: "Twenty-one days, Chi. That's not nothing."

*Chi lifts her cup and drinks on the number — a completed, unhurried movement, the
exact inverse of the interrupted one in Episode 2 Clip 4. It means: I am not going
to fight you about this. She says nothing about the number and the audience does
the arithmetic.*

**Nia arrives into the FRAME, not into the room.** She does not walk in from the
door and cross the café. Nine seconds of tracking one woman is what made ChiChi
the subject of Episode 2 Clip 1 and sent three of its four lines to her mouth.
Chi is in shot from frame one, so the two-shot is the subject from frame one.

### Clip 02 — "Right now" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 24s

> CHICHI: "What did he say?"
> NIA: "That he's not seeing anyone else."
> CHICHI: "Okay."
> *(beat)*
> NIA: "Right now. He's not seeing anyone else right now."
> CHICHI: "Mm."
> NIA: "Don't do that. That's a normal thing to say, that's how people talk. You don't sit there on a Tuesday and go 'I will never see anyone else for the rest of my life.' You say 'right now.' It's normal."
> CHICHI: "Okay."

*The two words are the episode. Chi never attacks them — she says "Okay" twice,
doing completely different work each time, and lets Nia argue with herself. Forty
words against one is section 1's rule exactly: length is Nia's tell.*

⚠️ **"Okay." appears twice and "right now" four times.** Adjacent lines sharing a
word is where attribution slips — it is what broke Episode 2 Clip 1, where lines 3
and 4 both ended on "midnight". Name the wrong answers in the prompt: never give
ChiChi the line *"Right now. He's not seeing anyone else right now."*, it is Nia's.

### Clip 03 — "I said cool" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 19s

> CHICHI: "What did you say back?"
> NIA: "What do you mean?"
> CHICHI: "When he said it. What did you say?"
> *(beat)*
> NIA: "...I said 'cool.'"
> CHICHI: "Mm."
> NIA: "I said cool because I was being cool. Because if I'd said anything else it would've been a whole thing, and it wasn't a whole thing. It was a nice night."

*Chi's weapon is the question, not the verdict. She does not tell Nia anything in
this clip; she asks one thing twice and Nia convicts herself. The only outright
laugh in the episode is "I said cool because I was being cool" and it has to be
played completely straight.*

### Clip 04 — "Available" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 27s · ENDS ON A HOLD

> NIA: "Just say it."
> CHICHI: "You don't want me to."
> NIA: "I'm asking you to say it. You've had the face on since I sat down, so say it, because I'd rather hear it than look at it."
> CHICHI: "You want me to say it so you can decide I'm wrong."
> NIA: "Chi."
> *(beat — Chi sets her cup down. One deliberate movement.)*
> CHICHI: "He's not lying to you. He's available."
> NIA: "That's a good thing. That's what you want. That's what everybody wants."
> CHICHI: "To who?"

***This clip is the episode.*** *The whole thing turns on one word meaning two
things. Nia hears "available" as single. Chi means still shopping. Neither of them
explains it and the prompt must not have anyone explain it. "To who?" ends the
exchange in two words and the clip holds on it — no reply, no reaction shot, no
music swell.*

**Chi sets her cup down before her verdict** — she is still, so her one deliberate
movement in twenty-seven seconds is the loudest thing she does, and it lands right
before the most useful sentence she says. Same mechanic as the glass on the rail in
Episode 2 Clip 5.

⚠️ **Longest clip in the episode at 27s and the most dangerous.** Eight lines
alternating between two women is the Episode 2 Clip 1 shape. The staging is Clip 5's
because that is what resolved it. Do not add a cut here to "help" — that is what was
tried three times and it never once worked.

### Clip 05 — "By now" · 50mm · f/2.2 · no cuts · 25s

> NIA: "When did you last get it wrong?"
> CHICHI: "What?"
> NIA: "About a man. When did you last get it wrong about a man?"
> *(Chi says nothing. The silence is declared and it belongs to her.)*
> NIA: "Because I'd rather get it wrong. I'd rather get it wrong twice a year than sit in a beautiful flat being right."
> *(beat — that went further than Nia meant it to)*
> NIA: "...Chi—"
> CHICHI: "No. You're right."
> *(beat)*
> CHICHI: "I'd like to have gotten it wrong with someone by now."

*Nia's one accurate hit in three episodes, and it is not cruel — it is what comes
out when she is cornered, and she hears it land and tries to take it back. Chi does
not retaliate and does not perform being hurt. She agrees. "A beautiful flat being
right" is Episode 1's empty sectional, named out loud.*

**This is the blind spot.** Chi envies Nia's ability to get it wrong. Played flat
and factual — never wistful, never tearful, never a confession. She says it the way
she says everything else and then it is over.

### Clip 06 — "You can look" · 50mm · f/2.2 · slow push · 11s · 5 WORDS

**The phone buzzes, face-up on the marble between them.** Both of them see it.
Nia turns it face-down without looking at it.

> CHICHI: "You can look."
> NIA: "I know."

*Beat. Nia does not look. Beat. **Then she looks.** Cut to black.*

*Section 1: end one beat earlier than feels comfortable. We never find out what the
message says, we never see Chi react to Nia looking, and the episode does not
comment. The engine is Chi tells Nia the truth, Nia hears it, Nia does it anyway —
so the last image in the episode is Nia doing it anyway.*

**Ends on NIA, not on Chi.** A reaction shot of Chi would tell the audience what to
think, which is the comment-versus-reaction line in section 3 one level up. The push
leaves the two-shot only AFTER the last word is spoken, exactly as Episode 2 Clip 5
did it — there is no attribution risk in a passage with no dialogue in it.

## THE SEAM MAP

Every seam in this episode is the same seam: **one continuous conversation, one
table, no time passing.** Section 4a says elapsed time is either legible or absent;
here it is absent, so every join must be continuous and nothing may change.

| Seam | Leaves | Inherits |
|---|---|---|
| 01 → 02 | Chi lowering her cup after the drink on "twenty-one days" | Cup down, Chi's hand off it, identical posture |
| 02 → 03 | On Nia, after Chi's second "Okay." | Identical two-shot, coffee one step lower |
| 03 → 04 | On Nia, having talked herself into a corner | Same, coffee lower |
| 04 → 05 | Held on Chi after "To who?", her cup now ON the table where she set it | **The cup stays where she set it** — she does not pick it up again |
| 05 → 06 | Chi has just said the most honest thing she has said | Nothing has moved. The buzz is the only event. |

**Props to track:** two coffee cups, going down and never refilled; Nia's phone,
face-up from the first frame and face-down from Clip 6; Chi's gold watch; nothing
else on the table.

**Section 4a's cut-continuity rule does not apply within this episode** — there is
no arrival, no exit and no change of place, so "open on whoever did not close the
previous clip" is replaced by "the two-shot never moves." That is a deliberate
exception and the reason is that the locked two-shot is also the attribution fix.

## WHAT IS NOT IN THIS EPISODE, AND WHY

- **Dorian does not appear.** He is in every clip and on screen in none. Putting him
  in costs a set, a wardrobe, a prose voice recipe and the three-way attribution
  risk, and it would turn a scene about what Nia tells herself into a scene about
  him. `Dorian-Series-Look` `51c0cbaa` stays locked and unused.
- **No new voice element.** See the registry.
- **No phone screen is ever shown.** The Episode 2 Clip 7 notes are explicit about
  how strong the model's prior is for putting a message on a phone. The buzz is
  heard, the screen is never seen, and the content of the message is never known —
  which is also the better ending.
