# EXCLUSIVE — THE NEW ORDER
## Season bible · Episodes N1–N4 · written 15 Sep 2026

The numbering starts over here. **`ep-n1-white.md` → `ep-n4-have-you.md` are the
new Episodes 1–4** and are the ones to shoot. The three existing files —
`ep-eleven-days.md`, `ep-the-caterer.md`, `ep-available.md` — are **not deleted
and not contradicted.** They become the PROLOGUE: optional viewing that makes the
new order hit harder, because the new order's whole engine is a fact that was
already true in every frame of them and nobody knew it.

Nothing in the old registries is retired. Every set this season needs is already
locked. See "What this season costs" below — the answer is unusually good.

---

## THE ONE NEW FACT — the engine of the season

**Chi knew Dorian. Two years before Nia ever said his name. Four months of him.
She has never told Nia, and on the night of the phone call she had about four
seconds to say it and did not.**

That is it. That is the whole season. Everything else is consequence.

### Why it costs nothing and is worth everything

It is a **retcon that adds, and removes nothing.** Go back and read the prologue
with it switched on:

| Prologue moment | What it was | What it is now |
|---|---|---|
| Ep "Eleven Days", Shot 2 — **"Who is he?"** | Chi being perceptive | Chi buying a second to decide whether to lie. **She already knew.** |
| Shot 5 — Chi closes her eyes, looks around the empty apartment, and the ask "lands somewhere it wasn't aimed" | her loneliness | that, **and** the trapdoor opening under her |
| Shot 6 — **"I'm leaving at midnight."** | a boundary | an exit strategy, planned before she arrives |
| Ep "The Caterer", Clip 2 — **"You must be Chi. I've heard a lot." / "Have you."** | a dry deflection | **two people performing a first meeting in front of a woman they are both lying to.** Dorian started it. Chi's "Have you." is a warning shot. |
| Clip 4 — Dorian: "this is my friend Nia" | the episode's wound | Chi's drink stopping halfway is **recognition**, not surprise. She has heard him do this before. |
| Clip 5 — **"Twelve." / "I'm not counting to win."** | Chi's tell about her own loneliness | **She was never counting Nia's days. She was counting her own silence.** Pays off in N4 Clip 5. |
| Clip 6 — **"You asked me to be in the room. I was in the room."** | a clean exit | the most defensible sentence she could construct, and she had all night to build it |
| Ep "Available" — Chi asks **"Are you exclusive?"** | the title as a question | **the only question she actually needs answered**, and not for Nia's sake |

No line of delivered footage has to change. Roughly 1,400 credits of Episode 2
and 3 picture **gains a second meaning for free.** That is the cheapest dramatic
asset this series will ever be handed.

### The rule that protects it

**Nobody explains any of the above, ever.** Not in dialogue, not in a look to
camera. The audience does the work. If a line has to point at the retcon for it
to land, the line is wrong.

## WHAT CHANGED IN THE RULES — read against CLAUDE.md §1

The user's brief is a harder show: cheating, betrayal, two-timing, a villain,
nasty talk, and three physical moments. Three guardrails move. **Four do not, and
the ones that hold are what keep this a drama instead of a soap.**

### MOVED

**1. "No villains" → THE VILLAIN IS CHI, AND SHE IS STILL A LEAD.**
The rule existed so no cartoon antagonist could walk in and let everyone else off
the hook. That danger is avoided here in the strongest possible way: the villain
is the source of truth. Chi does nothing loud. Her whole crime is a silence she
renewed every day for three weeks, and her own explanation of it (N4 C5) is the
most sympathetic speech in the series. **Play her as right up until the moment she
isn't**, exactly as always.

**The men stay off the hook and that part of the rule is untouched.** Dorian never
lies — he omits, and he is honest the second he is asked, which is worse and more
true. Kel is the most decent person on screen. Simone is not cruel; she is
*delighted*, which is a different and better register.

**2. "Chi gets fewer words than Nia, always" → BROKEN EXACTLY ONCE.**
N4 Clip 5. It is the longest speech Chi has had in seven episodes and it is the
payoff of six of them. **Its power is 100% borrowed from the rule being absolute
everywhere else**, so the rule is enforced harder than ever in N1–N3: Chi has
fewer words than Nia in every single clip that is not N4 C5. If she runs long
anywhere else, N4 C5 is worth nothing.

**3. Physical acts are now in the show.** Wine, water, a slap — one per episode
from N1, escalating, each landing on a different person. See "The three physical
moments" below; they have real production consequences and are not free.

### HELD — do not relax these because the season got nastier

- **Never moralise.** Nobody is punished with a lesson. Chi is not condemned by
  the show, Nia is not condemned for what she does in N3, and nobody says "you
  brought this on yourself." The audience supplies the wince. **This matters more
  now, not less** — a betrayal story that moralises is a sermon.
- **Laugh with them, never at them.** N2 is genuinely warm and funny. N3 Clip 2 is
  funny. Never once at Nia's expense.
- **The embarrassing thing is said plainly.** "He was at mine Wednesday." "It's a
  queue." No euphemism.
- **Specific beats universal.** "You're the only one who knew where the glasses
  were" is the season in one line. "She has trust issues" is not a line.
- **Chi is right, but not correct about everything.** Her blind spot this season is
  total and it is the plot: she believes silence is a form of protection. N4 C5 is
  her finding out she was protecting herself.

## CAST

### The two, unchanged

**NIA — 30.** Deep warm brown skin, waist-length jet-black water-wave curls,
diamond studs, thin gold choker. Warm, low, British-accented, dry and deadpan —
fast and bright when she wants something. Fresh, quick, charming, reliably wrong
about men. **Length is her tell: she talks fast when she is losing.**

*New this season:* she stops being only the one it happens to. In N3 she runs two
men on two phones and calls it maths. **She is never played as a fool for it and
never punished for it** — she is played as someone who has correctly learned the
wrong lesson.

**CHI — 40.** Warm brown complexion, almond dark eyes, full brows, cheek beauty
mark, stud earrings, full-figured. Warm, smooth, mid-to-low General American. Calm
authority, dry as a bone. Wants a child, has no partner, least direct about that.

*Series look, hair rules, the NO-RING rule, the gold watch, the stillness* — all
unchanged, all in CLAUDE.md §3, all still absolute. **The NO-RING rule especially:
she is the other woman this season and a wedding ring on her hand would blow the
entire premise, not just continuity.**

### The men, kept and played with

**DORIAN — 38.** `Dorian-Series-Look` `51c0cbaa-2fbe-4918-bb91-a29307caca09`.
6'2", a clear 4–5 inches taller than Chi, all-black camp-collar set, silver chain
and cross, whiskey tumbler.

*What changes:* nothing about how he is played. He is still warm, unhurried, and
genuinely likeable — **the episode depends on that, same as it always did.** What
changes is what we know: he has been seeing Simone for three years, on and off,
mostly on. He has never lied about it to anyone. He just does not bring it up.
When Nia finally asks him straight, he answers straight and it is devastating.
**He is not a villain and must never be played as one.** Ordinary and unavailable.

**KEL — 50s.** `Kel-The-Cousin` `3ad69411-e32c-44bd-b797-a93ec0d4b52c`. Dorian's
older cousin. Voice target **106.7Hz, warmth 38** — prose recipe, no element.

*What changes, and this is the good one:* the old rule was **"Kel does not know he
is the punchline and never finds out."** The new order keeps him from ever finding
out — and reveals that he was never the punchline. **Kel is the sharpest read in
the room and the only person all season who says a true thing without an angle.**
He gets a whole episode (N2), he has real chemistry with Chi, he clocks her in
about nine minutes, and he lets her lie to him anyway because he likes her. He is
the moral floor of the show. Still generous, still funny, still makes you a plate.

### The one new speaker

**SIMONE — 34.** Dorian's other relationship. Three years, on and off, mostly on.

**Her register is the whole point and it is easy to get wrong.** She is not shrill,
not a rival, never raises her voice, never looks messy. She is **cheerful**. She
does devastating things pleasantly and at a completely normal volume, and she is
having a lovely time. The most frightening person in the season pours the wine the
way you would water a plant.

She is also **not cruel to Nia**, which is what makes her work — in N4 she hands
Nia the truth kindly, and being kind about it is worse than any insult available.

⚠️ **She needs a reference image and a prose voice recipe. She cannot have a voice
element** — the slots are full at three and none is disposable (CLAUDE.md §5a).
See the manifest.

### Seen never, heard never, costs nothing

**ELIAS.** Nia's second man in N3. **He is never on screen and never speaks.** He
exists entirely in Nia's description of him — "a grown man with a car and a mother
he calls" — which is funnier, cheaper and more damning than any casting. Zero
credits, zero references, zero voice. Keep him that way.

**The escalation is deliberate and it has a shape:** the wine is thrown by a
stranger at a stranger, the water by a friend at a friend, the slap by the person
with the most right to. Each one is more intimate and less forgivable than the
last, and **each one is caught by someone who does not move.**

### The two motifs that run all four

**"That's not the same sentence."** People distinguishing between two things that
sound identical. Dorian starts it in N1 C3 ("she looks happy" / "she is happy"),
Simone does it in N1 C5, **Kel aims it straight at Chi in N2 C4 without knowing**
("that's the same thing" / "is it?"), and it is the shape of Chi's entire defence.

**The counting.** Eleven days → twelve → twenty-one → and in N4 Clip 4 **Nia slaps
her on the word "twelve."** The prologue's running joke becomes the season's worst
moment. This is the single best beat in the four scripts and everything else bends
to protect it.

## THE THREE PHYSICAL MOMENTS — production, not style

These were written the way they are **because of what the model can and cannot
render.** Do not "improve" them back into the obvious version.

### 🍷 The wine is POURED, slowly. It is not thrown.

A throw is a fast ballistic event: the model gets one frame of ambiguous liquid and
a lot of smeared arm. **A slow pour is continuous, gravity-driven, has no impact
moment, and stays in contact with the bottle the entire time** — which is the kind
of motion that renders. It is also scarier. Simone maintains eye contact and pours
it like she is watering a plant.

⚠️ **The stain is then a permanent wardrobe fact.** It is the last clip of N1 so
nothing follows it — but if a clip is ever added after, the dress is stained in it,
and `Nia-White-Dress` must be shot with that in mind. Never let the stain "settle"
between takes into a different shape mid-clip.

### 💧 The water is NOT rendered in flight. Shoot the two ends.

Cut on **Nia's hand closing on the glass and the arm starting up.** Cut away on the
first frame of the throw. Come back on **Chi already wet.** The liquid in the air
is the one thing in the sequence with no chance of rendering cleanly; a wet face is
a *state*, held and still, which is exactly what the model is good at and exactly
what Chi does anyway. The sound carries the middle.

**Do not ask for a splash.** Ask for: water running off the jaw, one strand of hair
stuck to her cheek, a dark patch spreading on the shoulder of the sweater, a puddle
on the table, and absolute stillness.

### 🤚 The slap: never render the contact.

Two character elements touching is where faces break, and an impact frame is the
worst possible instance of it. **Cut on the windup.** The sound lands over black or
over the incoming frame. **Open the next clip on the aftermath** — Chi's head still
turned, one cheek darker, not moving.

Chi not moving after it is the performance, and it is a still frame, which costs
nothing and cannot drift. **Nia's hand is what we see, not Chi's face taking it.**

## WHAT THIS SEASON COSTS — read before choosing a tier

**378 seconds** of finished picture across four episodes — 94 + 92 + 92 + 100,
measured from the clip tables, not estimated.

| Tier | Rate | Clean run | Realistic, with re-shoots |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1080p** | 9 cr/s | **~3,400 credits** | **~6,000–9,000** |
| **480p** | 2.5 cr/s | **~945 credits** | ~1,600–2,300 |

Per episode at 1080p: **N1 ~846 · N2 ~828 · N3 ~828 · N4 ~900.**

⚠️ **The clean number is not the number.** Episode 2 ran roughly 1,420 credits for
70 seconds — about 2.25× its clean 1080p estimate — and Episode 3 re-shot Clip 5
four times and Clip 6 three times. **Budget 2–3×.**

⚠️ **The balance will not cover four episodes at 1080p.** It was 1,769.59 after the
Episode 3 plate and Episode 3 has shot six clips since. **Run `balance` before
committing to a tier, and commit to the tier once** — CLAUDE.md §7 forbids swapping
resolution mid-episode, and the only free window for that decision is before the
first clip.

**The recommendation: shoot N1 at 1080p and decide the rest from what it costs.**
N1 is the pilot of the new order, it carries the wine, and it is the one that has
to look expensive. Episode 1 of the old order is 480p and is never cut against
these, so the tiers do not have to match across the season — only within an episode.
