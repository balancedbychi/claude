## THE TWO STORIES

### NIA'S — "the exclusive"

Dorian says the word. There is a Simone. Nia finds out inside ninety seconds and
**does not leave** — she gets even. She adds a second man, runs two phones, calls
it parity, and for about one episode she is the villain of her own story: she
texts the other woman to a public café and throws water on her.

She is never played as a fool and never punished. She is a woman who has learned
exactly the wrong lesson at speed and is defending it brilliantly. In N4 she finally
asks Dorian the question straight, makes him say the name, and slaps him on it.

### CHI'S — "the caterer's cousin and the lawyer"

Chi and Kel start dating. It is the warmest thing the series has done — he cooks in
her flat, she owns a second glass, she wears the red. He is fifty-three, has a grown
daughter, and when she asks him sideways whether he is done, he says his age and
nothing else.

Then Graham. A successful white lawyer at the next table at Sucré, who bills in
six-minute increments and reads a room correctly. He is direct, he is funny, and on
the first afternoon he says two things: he wants kids, and he is separated. Eleven
months. "It's civil."

**Chi has told Nia a hundred times that separated is married.** She goes to dinner
anyway. That is the show's engine turned on Chi for the first time — *she tells the
truth, she hears it, she does it anyway* — and it is her blind spot for the season.
She is never a villain. She is a woman who wants a child and has just been offered
it by the wrong man, while the right one says "fifty-three."

### Where they meet

Every episode opens or closes with the two of them on the phone or across the
table, and **for the first time in the series Nia is sometimes the one with the
advice.** "Separated is married, Chi. You taught me that." The inversion is the
freshness. **It never becomes a rift.** N4 ends with Nia asleep on Chi's sofa.

## WHO IS WHO

**NIA — 30.** Unchanged. Deep warm brown skin, waist-length jet-black water-wave
curls, diamond studs, gold choker. British, low, dry, fast when she is losing.
Kinetic. Length is her tell.

**CHI — 40.** Unchanged. Warm brown complexion, almond dark eyes, cheek beauty mark,
full-figured. American, lower, dry, economical. Still. Wants a child. **No ring,
ever** — this season it is a plot fact as well as a continuity one. Series look,
hair rules and the gold watch per CLAUDE.md §3, all still absolute.

**DORIAN — 38.** `Dorian-Series-Look` `51c0cbaa`. 6'2", all black, silver chain,
whiskey. Warm, unhurried, genuinely likeable. **Never a villain.** He does not lie.
He does not volunteer. When Nia finally asks him straight he answers straight, and
his last line — "I never lied to you" — is true and is the whole problem.

**KEL — 53.** `Kel-The-Cousin` `3ad69411`. Dorian's older cousin. Voice target
106.7Hz, warmth 38 — prose recipe. The old rule was that he never finds out he is
the punchline. **He still never finds out, because he never was one.** He cooks, he
counts, he is the sharpest read in the room and the only person in the season with
no angle. He has a daughter, 24, in Atlanta. He is the available man. **He is the
one Chi is not choosing, and the show has no opinion about that.**

**GRAHAM — 44. NEW.** A successful white lawyer. Mid-baritone, precise, clipped
consonants, General American, dry, no drawl, no fry — **he must sit between Kel and
Dorian in pitch and separate from both by timbre**: Dorian is round and chesty, Kel
is bright and thin, Graham is clean and articulate, courtroom clarity. He is not a
saviour, not a creep, not a tourist. He sees what Chi sees — when the water goes
over Simone, his read is "I'd take that case. Hers." — and that shared accuracy is
the attraction. His unavailability is the ordinary kind: separated, not divorced.

**SIMONE — 34. NEW.** Dorian's other woman. Three years, on and off, mostly on.
**Cheerful.** Never shrill, never messy, never raises her voice. Does devastating
things pleasantly and at normal volume. Pours the wine the way you would water a
plant. Takes the water and says "Tuesday, then." She wins every scene she is in and
enjoys all of them.

**ELIAS.** Nia's second man. **Never seen, never heard.** "A grown man with a car
and a mother he calls." Zero credits, zero references. Keep him that way.

### The motifs

- **Counting.** Eleven, twelve, twenty-one, Tuesday, Wednesday, six-minute
  increments, fifty-three. Everyone in this show keeps score.
- **"That's not an answer."** Chi says it to Kel in N2. Nia says it to Chi in N4
  about the same non-answer. *"That's what I said."* They are the same person ten
  years apart, and that is the show.
- **Two glasses.** The series has had one glass on Chi's marble table since the
  first shot. **N2 puts a second one there and it is the happiest image in seven
  episodes.** N4 ends with two glasses, Nia asleep, and a foil-wrapped plate on the
  doormat.

## WHAT THIS SEASON DOES TO THE RULES — read against CLAUDE.md §1

**Moved:**
- **Physical acts are in the show.** Wine, water, a slap. See below — each is
  written around what the renderer can hold.
- **"No villains" bends for Nia, for one episode, and never for anyone else.** N3
  is Nia's villain stretch and it is a stretch, not a role — she is out of it by N4
  C3. Chi is never one. The men are ordinary and unavailable, as always. Simone is
  not a villain; she is the wronged party who is cheerfully winning.
- **The audience is wider.** The season has one white character and he is a love
  interest. **The texture is in the specifics, never in a speech** — "He's white,
  Chi." / "I have eyes, Nia." / "I'm sitting right here." / "I know, that's why I
  said it." Kel's "Is it because he's—" / "Don't." / "I was going to say a lawyer."
  Both audiences laugh, nobody is lectured, nobody is a symbol.

**Held, and they matter more now:**
- **Never moralise.** Nobody is punished with a lesson — not Nia for the water, not
  Chi for Graham. The audience supplies the wince.
- **Laugh with them, never at them.**
- **The embarrassing thing is said plainly.** "He was at mine Wednesday." "He says
  he wants kids." "I just hit a man."
- **Chi gets fewer words than Nia, always.** Chi has no speech anywhere in the
  season. Her longest line is eight words. Hold it.
- **Chi is right but not correct about everything.** Her blind spot is Graham, and
  Nia names it.
- **No exposition between them.** They already know everything; the audience
  catches up.

## THE THREE PHYSICAL MOMENTS — production, not style

Written this way because of what the model can and cannot render. **Do not
"improve" them back into the obvious version.**

- 🍷 **The wine is POURED, slowly. Never thrown.** A throw is a fast ballistic
  event; a pour is continuous, gravity-driven, in contact with the bottle the whole
  time — that renders. It is also scarier. The stain is then a permanent wardrobe
  fact for every later frame of the episode.
- 💧 **The water is never rendered in flight.** Cut on the arm's upswing; come back
  on Simone already wet — water off the jaw, one strand of hair stuck, a dark patch
  on the shoulder, a puddle. A wet face is a *state*, and states hold. The sound
  carries the middle. Do not ask for a splash.
- 🤚 **The slap: never render the contact.** Two character elements touching is
  where faces break. Cut on the windup; land on the aftermath — Dorian's head
  turned, one cheek darker, not touching it.
