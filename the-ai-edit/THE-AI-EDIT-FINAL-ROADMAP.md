# The AI Edit — Higgsfield Creator Course Roadmap (Final)

Foundation, specialist courses, membership tiers, and monthly content plan for a Skool membership that teaches people to build with AI on purpose, without wasting money.

Updated September 23, 2026. This is the complete roadmap. It combines the original course plan with research on Higgsfield's current models, tools, voice workflow, Supercomputer, and the Claude connection, plus a Claude vs ChatGPT model guide. It is a curriculum and production plan. The courses, reference packs, and templates still need to be produced and tested.

**Member outcome:** Choose a creative direction, build a consistent character (look *and* voice) and reusable scene assets, produce a finished piece of content on a known budget, then develop one chosen content or business path.

**Teaching principle:** One direction, one character or format, one small project at a time. Test cheap, finish expensive. Members can explore the full library while following a focused route.

**How facts were checked:** Higgsfield model names, inputs, and credit costs were pulled from the live Higgsfield catalog and its cost-check tool through the connected account on September 23, 2026. No credits were spent. Other facts come from the sources listed at the end. Costs and model names change often, so re-check them within 30 days of recording any lesson.

## 2. Teach the different creative decisions separately

A character's identity, visual appearance, voice, cinematic treatment, content format, and business model are different decisions. Teach this before students collect prompts.

| Decision | Examples | Question the student answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | An authorized twin of yourself; a fictional avatar; a recurring brand character | Who is appearing? |
| Visual appearance | Photorealistic; stylized realistic; 3D cartoon; 2D illustrated/anime-inspired | What should the character look like? |
| Voice | Your own cloned voice; a preset voice; a permitted voice | What does the character sound like? |
| Visual treatment | Cinematic; casual phone-camera; editorial; studio/product | How should the scene be lit, framed, and presented? |
| Content format | Dance; beauty transition; UGC-style ad; short drama; narrated faceless story | What will the content do? |
| Distribution and income | Audience building; brand services; affiliate sales; paid stories; platform programs | Where will it go, and what is the business path? |

Cinematic does not mean a different kind of avatar: a realistic person and an animated character can both be presented cinematically. UGC describes a content format and presentation convention. An AI-made UGC-style ad is synthetic creative, not evidence of a real customer experience.

Also distinguish three different things people call a "model":
- an AI character (the on-screen "model")
- the generation model you select in Higgsfield (Seedance 2.5, Soul 2.0…)
- the assistant model you plan with (Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol…)

Teach with the same simple subject across several visual examples. Compare skin/material treatment, proportions, lighting, framing, and intended audience. Students select one direction for their first project.

## 3. Launch curriculum: Foundation Modules 0–7 (Basic and Advanced)

All eight modules, their essential resources, and a completed instructor example should be ready before launch.

| Module | Course | Required member output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Choose Your Creative Direction | A one-page direction brief with a budget |
| 1 | Understand AI Looks and Art Direction | A selected visual style, a small reference board, and the matching Higgsfield model |
| 2 | Plan with ChatGPT or Claude, Organize with Claude Code | A production brief, a shot list with model and voice route per shot, and organized project documents |
| 3 | Higgsfield Foundations and Credit Control | A deliberate test with documented settings and costs |
| 4 | Build Your Character: Look and Voice | An approved character kit (reference pack, Element/Soul ID, Voice ID, voice notes) |
| 5 | Build Your Set | An approved environment with consistent reference views, saved as an Element |
| 6 | Build and Manage Your Assets | A reusable asset library with Higgsfield IDs and a continuity checklist |
| 7 | Direct and Finish Your First Scene | A finished short piece with one line of dialogue, plus a production and cost review |

Suggested scope: about 36 short lessons, usually 5–10 minutes each, plus practice. These are planning targets, not completion-time guarantees. Keep tool-installation walkthroughs as support lessons so they don't overwhelm the creative sequence.

### Module 0 — Choose Your Creative Direction

Three lessons:

1. Choose one first goal: an avatar account, a branded scene, a short story, or a faceless format.
2. Identify the audience, purpose, preferred platform, visual direction, time available, and **generation budget** (Higgsfield plan, monthly credits, first-project budget).
3. Choose a first deliverable and put other ideas on a "Later" list.

Assignment: complete the direction brief and choose one small project to finish over the first month. Completion is based on the output, not a forced pace.

Resource: direction worksheet with fields for audience, identity type, look, voice, format, first output, budget, tool access, and later ideas.

Completion check: the member can say exactly what they will make next, what it should cost, and what is outside that project's scope.

### Module 1 — Understand AI Looks and Art Direction

Five lessons:

1. Compare photorealistic, stylized realistic, 3D cartoon, and 2D illustrated looks.
2. Compare cinematic, casual phone-camera, editorial, and studio treatments.
3. Select references for texture, color, lighting, composition, and wardrobe; explain each reference's role.
4. Write a simple visual direction brief and identify conflicting style instructions.
5. **Look → model crosswalk:** which Higgsfield models tend to produce each look.

| Look / treatment | Start with | Alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photorealistic, phone-camera UGC, fashion | Soul 2.0 | Nano Banana Pro |
| Cinematic | Cinema Studio Image 2.5 | Soul Cinema (trained character) |
| Stylized, 3D, illustrated | Nano Banana Pro with style references | Grok Image (bold), Z Image (fast drafts) |
| Editorial, studio, product | Soul 2.0, Marketing Studio Image | Nano Banana Pro |
| Text, logos, thumbnails | GPT Image 2.5 | OpenAI Hazel, Recraft V4.1 |

Assignment: create a small reference board, choose one appearance plus one treatment, and name the model you'll test first. State what should remain consistent.

Resources: visual style comparison guide; reference-role worksheet; style selection checklist; look → model crosswalk.

Completion check: the student can explain the intended look without relying on a long list of vague adjectives.

### Module 2 — Plan with ChatGPT or Claude, Organize with Claude Code

Five lessons:

1. Use ChatGPT or Claude to turn the direction brief into a scene concept, short script, and list of shots. Members only need one paid assistant, or a free tier, to start.
2. Write specific instructions: context, task, references, constraints, desired output, and a targeted revision. Ask the assistant what is ambiguous before spending credits.
3. **Which assistant model for which job.** Teach roles, not just names: fast (Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5.6 Luna/Instant), balanced (Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Sol Medium), and deep thinking (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol High / GPT-6 Pro). See Appendix D.
4. Store approved project information and keep drafts separate from material ready for generation.
5. Use Claude Code to organize a small folder and update project documents; review the actual changes.

Assignment: create a production brief, character outline, and organized project documents, plus a shot list with the columns `shot | model | test or final | voice route | estimated credits`.

Resources: brief template; prompt framework; shot-list-with-routes template; starter folder example; sample project instructions.

ChatGPT Projects can group related chats, uploaded sources, and instructions. [Official OpenAI documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/projects).

Claude Code can work on files and run commands. Teach it as a workspace with reviewable actions, and distinguish it from ordinary Claude chat. Its CLAUDE.md guidance supplies context rather than enforced behavior. [Claude Code overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/overview), [project memory documentation](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory).

Provide the same documents as a manual route for Basic members who are not ready to adopt Claude Code. The direct Claude → Higgsfield connection is taught in Module 8 (Advanced).

Note for lessons: OpenAI discontinued the Sora app on April 26, 2026, and the Sora API ends September 24, 2026. ChatGPT is a planning tool in this course, not a video tool.

Completion check: the student can find approved material, understand what was changed, and resume the project.

### Module 3 — Higgsfield Foundations and Credit Control

Six lessons:

1. **The Higgsfield floor plan (overview):** the website, Supercomputer (Higgsfield's built-in AI agent), and the connector for Claude/ChatGPT. Beginners work on the website. See Appendix C.
2. Navigate the current interface, assets, generation settings, and usage history.
3. **Choose a model for the task:** read its supported inputs (start/end frame, image references, video references, audio references), lengths, and controls. Use the model cheat sheet (Appendix A).
4. **The model ladder: test cheap → confirm → finish expensive.** Example: Kling 3.0 Turbo test (7.5 credits for 5 s) → Kling 3.0 (10) → Seedance 2.5 final (35 for 5 s at 720p; 120 for 10 s at 1080p).
5. **The five cost levers:** model, duration, resolution, quality tier, and audio on/off. Inspect the displayed cost and run a small test that answers one specific question.
6. Review the result and decide whether to keep, correct, simplify, switch approach, or stop.

Assignment: record one test and the reason for the next decision.

Resources: model cheat sheet (Appendix A); generation log; project spending worksheet; before-you-generate checklist.

Log the tool and route, model, prompt version, references, duration/resolution, audio setting, charge, refund if any, usability, and next step. Track total net credits per usable output rather than highlighting only the best attempt.

Higgsfield documents that costs vary with generation settings and that eligible website Unlimited/free allowances do not extend to connected agent/CLI generations. Subscription credits reset and do not roll over. Confirm the current account, plan, and route before teaching specific costs. [Credit guidance](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/credits/how-credits-work), [how plans work](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/plans/how-do-higgsfield-plans-work).

Completion check: the student can find the actual cost, name which lever to change, and explain whether another attempt is justified.

### Module 4 — Build Your Character: Look and Voice

Six lessons:

1. Choose an authorized real-person twin or a fictional identity and define the character's purpose.
2. Build the character guide: face, complexion, body proportions, hair, age range, wardrobe, and visual style.
3. Prepare identity references, portraits, full-body views, and useful angles, without treating generated variants as automatically correct. Higgsfield's character-sheet workflow can build turnaround and expression sheets.
4. **Soul ID vs Elements.** Train a Soul ID (5–20 photos of one person, about 10 minutes; works only with Soul 2.0 and Soul Cinema) for an AI twin or single recurring influencer. Save an Element (instant, from one or more images) to carry the character into other image models and into video, including shots with several characters. Most members need an Element; many will want both.
5. Generate the approved first image and diagnose changes to the face, body, hair, hands, or clothing.
6. **"Your character is two assets: a look and a voice."** Create a Voice from your own recording or a permitted voice (Higgsfield asks for consent). Write voice notes: language, accent, age, energy, pace, and words the character would never say. Explain that **video models do not carry the voice automatically.** Soul IDs and Elements hold only the look, and video models such as Seedance 2.5 can switch audio on or off but cannot select a saved voice. The voice is brought in as audio or swapped in afterward (Appendix B).

Assignment: assemble one approved character kit and demonstrate the character in a second setup. The kit contains the reference pack, Element ID and/or Soul ID, Voice ID, and voice notes.

Resources: character guide; angle/reference checklist; Soul ID vs Elements decision card; voice notes template; character quality-review sheet.

Teach consistent identity as a result to inspect, not a guarantee from a "lock" phrase. Use your own or authorized face and voice references. Explain the difference between rights to use an asset and rights to distribute the reference or prompt to other people.

Completion check: the member can identify unwanted changes, explain which references or decisions need revisiting, and say where the character's voice comes from in a video.

### Module 5 — Build Your Set

Four lessons:

1. Define the location's purpose: a vanity for beauty, a kitchen or tabletop for products, a podcast corner, or a room for a story.
2. Plan the layout, furniture, background objects, color palette, lighting direction, and space for the action.
3. Establish camera height, distance, framing, and screen direction. Create an empty-set reference with **Soul Location** (or Cinema Studio Image 2.5) before combining it with the character.
4. Build and inspect a small set of views (establishing/wide, medium, and a relevant alternate angle), then **save the approved set as an Element** so it can be carried into image and video models.

Assignment: produce one reusable set with a written description, approved reference views, and its Element ID.

Resources: set-building worksheet; simple layout guide; lighting and framing checklist; environment continuity sheet.

Check object placement, scale, shadows, architecture, and light direction across views. Generating a reverse angle does not guarantee the room's geometry stays consistent. Show how to identify and correct those errors.

Completion check: the student can place their character in the environment plausibly and explain what must remain consistent in the next shot.

### Module 6 — Build and Manage Your Assets

Four lessons:

1. List assets before generating: character views, wardrobe, props, products, backgrounds, stills, voices, and motion references.
2. Prepare references for recurring objects, clothing, and hero props. For real products, preserve accurate shape, packaging, labels, and scale. Save recurring props and products as Elements.
3. Give approved assets clear names and versions; label each item's purpose and source/permission.
4. Make a continuity checklist and reuse the approved material in a second scene.

Assignment: organize the character kit, set references, and a few relevant wardrobe/prop references into one reusable library. Asset count depends on the chosen project.

Resources: asset manifest; file-naming guide; reference-rights field; approved/draft/replaced status labels; continuity checklist.

Suggested asset record fields: name or ID, category, version, reference file, description, source/permission, **Higgsfield ID (Element / Soul / Voice)**, **models tested with**, **voice route** (for characters), intended role, approved date, linked shots, and known limitations.

Completion check: the student can locate the current version and ID of each required asset and explain what it controls in the scene.

### Module 7 — Direct and Finish Your First Scene

Five lessons:

1. Combine the character, set, assets, and shot plan into an approved still (start frame). Cinema Studio Image 2.5 or Nano Banana Pro with Elements works well here.
2. Animate one manageable action with a reference-capable model. Test on Kling 3.0 Turbo; finish on Seedance 2.5 or Kling 3.0.
3. **Add one line of dialogue with Route B:** generate the clip with native audio, then run Voice Change with the character's saved Voice. (Routes A and C are taught in Module 9.)
4. Review continuity, motion, speech, audio, and framing; document a targeted correction.
5. Assemble and export a short piece, prepare its caption, and review total effort, credits, and **credits per usable second**.

Assignment: finish a small scene, typically 15–30 seconds assembled from 5-second shots, with a shorter alternative when budget or tool access calls for it. A realistic budget is about 245 credits (Appendix E).

Resources: scene direction worksheet; shot review rubric; export checklist; production retrospective with cost summary.

Demonstrate the assembly method you actually use. Higgsfield's built-in editing (Higgsedit) and Subtitles tools can handle simple cuts, text, and burned-in captions. If a separate editor is needed, disclose it before the lesson and show a basic route. Public posting is optional.

Completion check: the result matches the original brief, and the student can explain how to repeat the process for a new idea and what it cost.

### Module 8 — Claude + Higgsfield: The Studio Folder

Five lessons:

1. **Connect.** In Claude, open Settings → Connectors, add Higgsfield, and sign in. No API key or code needed. Higgsfield also documents a CLI route for agents like Claude Code, and a ChatGPT connection. Check your credit balance before and after.
2. **Build the Studio Folder** and CLAUDE.md rules (Appendix C template).
3. **Generate from the shot list.** Claude reads the Element, Soul, and Voice IDs from your files and shows a cost preflight before each generation.
4. **Automatic logging.** Every generation becomes a row in the generation log.
5. **Limits.** The connector does not attach a voice to a character. Website Unlimited allowances generally do not cover connector/CLI generations. Conversational spending limits are not hard caps. Claude can't see your Higgsfield website history unless you give it IDs or files.

Higgsfield currently documents the connector and CLI routes and says conversational credit limits are not hard caps. [Higgsfield integration guidance](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/integrations/how-do-i-connect-higgsfield-to-ai-agent), [Higgsfield CLI](https://higgsfield.ai/cli).

Completion check: the member can run one generation from Claude using stored IDs, see its cost first, and find it in the log.

### Module 9 — Voice and Dialogue Production

Five lessons:

1. Why the voice doesn't stick: faces and voices are separate assets, and video models can't select a saved voice.
2. **Route A: voice first, audio reference.** Generate the line in Seed Audio with your Voice, then pass it as an audio reference to Seedance 2.5 (omni-reference mode) with the character Element.
3. **Route C: silent picture, exact voice, lip sync.** Generate the shot with audio off, generate the exact line, then run Sync Lipsync 3 (Lipsync Studio on the website).
4. **Two characters:** one Element and one Voice each, one speaker per shot, and a dialogue table (`shot | character | element_id | voice_id | line | route | credits | status`).
5. **Dubbing** into a second language (18 languages); consent and disclosure.

Before recording, test Route A on your own character. Higgsfield's Seedance guidance treats an audio reference as a voice-identity guide, so it may re-perform the line rather than reproduce it word for word. Label Route A Tested or Under Evaluation.

Completion check: the member can choose the right route for a scene and produce a dialogue exchange with consistent voices.

### Module 10 — Supercomputer and Automation with Guardrails

Four lessons:

1. **What Supercomputer is:** Higgsfield's AI agent, not hardware. You describe a goal; it plans, shows the combined credit cost for approval, then generates at normal credit rates. It has memory of your brand and past work. When to use it instead of Claude + connector (Appendix C).
2. **Skills Marketplace:** install one Skill (for example, product brief → UGC video) and read what it will generate and cost.
3. **Scheduled tasks:** set one up (for example, weekly ad variations) with a written monthly cap and a weekly review. Higgsfield lists up to 10 active scheduled tasks on Ultra.
4. **When not to automate:** identity drift, unreviewed product claims, and credit burn while you're away.

Completion check: the member can explain what their scheduled task will spend each month and how they review it.

### Month 1 — Dance Trends and Motion Direction

Teach how to:
- choose a movement that fits the avatar
- prepare a useful full-body reference
- obtain or record an authorized motion video
- match framing and starting pose
- evaluate hands, feet, floor contact, clothing, and background changes
- sync audio and check whether the soundtrack is permitted for the intended use

Higgsfield's current route is **Genjutsu motion transfer**: one or more character images plus one driving video, output at 480p, 720p, or 1080p. Kling 3.0 is the alternative for motion transfer. Verify the available model and controls during course production. The linked Kling 2.6 guide is useful for the method rather than as a permanent plan or pricing promise. [Higgsfield motion-control guide](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/Kling-2.6-Motion-Control-Full-Guide).

Separate the stable motion lesson from dated trend examples. A new dance example can replace an old one without rebuilding the course. Higgsfield's TikTok tools can surface trending music; check each sound's license. Do not promise exact choreography replication or viral reach.

Reference pack: motion brief, reference checklist, body-framing guide, timing notes, and failure examples.

### Month 2 — Beauty, Makeup, and Outfit Transitions

Teach:
- matched before/after assets and stable facial identity
- camera angle and position, and lighting continuity
- transition gestures, cuts on movement, and timing

Create before/after states with image edits (Nano Banana 2, which supports masked edits to one area, or Seedream 5.0 Lite for instruction-based edits). Demonstrate both an edited transition between approved shots and a generative transition using start and end frames (Seedance 2.5, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 Lite). Use Genjutsu replace object to swap an outfit or product in an existing clip.

Cover what must change and what must stay consistent. Inspect makeup, hair, clothes, jewelry, background, and product appearance. Present synthetic beauty transformations as creative effects, not proof that a product caused a result.

Reference pack: before/after planning sheet, transition storyboard, continuity checklist, and edit timing example.

### Month 3 — AI UGC and Product Content

Teach audience and product research, a short brief, hooks, accurate product information, the on-camera avatar or presenter, set choice, product close-ups, dialogue, and a clear call to action.

Higgsfield tools for this track:
- **Marketing Studio:** products, avatars, brand kits, hooks (the "what"), settings (the "where"), and Ad References (recreate the structure of an existing ad). Its video output runs 12–15 seconds.
- **UGC workflows:** review, unboxing, try-on, tutorial, product-only, and website/SaaS formats.
- **Ad Multiplier:** hook and variation testing.
- **Product photoshoot workflow:** stills.
- **Route C (exact voice + lip sync):** for approved scripts.

Build one room or tabletop set and reuse it in several shot types. Check packaging, labels, scale, hand-object contact, and what the real product can do. Include a client-ready delivery exercise and revision boundaries.

Reference pack: UGC brief, product fact sheet, set plan, shot list, script framework, review checklist, and sample delivery pack.

Do not present a fictional avatar as a real customer recounting an actual experience. Teach disclosure and product accuracy in the relevant platform context.

### Month 4 — Build a Faceless Content Format

Teach selection of one audience and original format, research and fact-checking where needed, scripting, narration, visuals, editing, and a manageable publishing system. Examples can include fictional stories, explainers, or themed visual narratives.

Higgsfield's faceless video workflow produces narrated multi-scene videos (explainer, history, kids, picture story, or fairy tale/myth). It locks a consistent non-photoreal style, reusable assets, one narrator voice, and burned subtitles. Use the thumbnail workflow for covers. Narration uses Seed Audio or Text to Speech V2 with a saved or preset voice; no lip sync needed.

Create three posts with distinct substance to test the format. Explain how to assess audience response and improve the next piece. Separate the ability to publish from eligibility for advertising or other income programs.

Reference pack: format brief, three-post content map, originality checklist, source log, and performance review sheet.

YouTube's monetization policy requires original, authentic value and addresses repetitive, mass-produced, and reused content. Faceless or AI-made content is not itself an income guarantee. [YouTube monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en-GB).

### Month 5 — AI Short Stories and Recurring Series

Teach a premise, character motivations, scene structure, emotional progression, dialogue coverage, episode continuity, and a manageable production plan.

Give every cast member an Element and a Voice. Use Cinema Studio Video 3.0 or Cinema Studio Video 2 (genre control, multi-shot with a prompt per shot) for cinematic scenes and Seedance 2.5 for reference-driven shots. Handle dialogue with Routes A and C, one speaker per shot.

Reuse characters and sets across scenes while making each episode meaningfully different. Use your own series as a documented example where it is ready to teach, including costs and unsuccessful attempts.

Reference pack: series guide, episode outline, scene breakdown, character/voice records with IDs, asset reuse map, dialogue table, and production log.

### Month 6 — Paid Story Libraries and YouTube Distribution

Teach the viewer journey: a free sample or trailer, a clear paid offer, sign-in, purchase, entitled access, cancellation, and access expiry. Compare YouTube channel memberships with an independently paid site using suitably controlled video hosting. Use Clipify to cut trailers and short clips from long episodes, and Dubbing to create language versions.

YouTube supports members-only viewing through channel memberships for eligible creators. Check eligibility when producing this lesson. [YouTube membership requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7636690?hl=en), [membership benefits](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14917424?hl=en-WS).

An unlisted YouTube embed is shareable by anyone who obtains the link. A paid page around it does not make the video itself securely restricted to paying viewers. Demonstrate this limitation and distinguish page access from video access. [YouTube privacy settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/157177?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en).

Assignment: map and test a small access flow with sample content. Test a paying viewer, a non-paying viewer, and revoked/expired access. Do not require students to charge real customers to complete the lesson.

Reference pack: offer worksheet, viewing journey, platform comparison, access test checklist, and expense tracker.

The tool or creator called "Bendable" in the discussion has not been verified. Keep this example pending a link or screenshot; do not silently substitute another product or teach an assumed feature.

### Month 7 — TikTok Shop and Affiliate Content

Teach the difference between earning affiliate commission, selling a product, providing creative services, and receiving platform creator payments. Verify current country and account eligibility, product requirements, disclosure, and content rules before students build a campaign.

Use real product facts and approved reference material. Build creative in Marketing Studio with approved scripts voiced through Route C. Teach testing different hooks (Ad Multiplier), tracking clicks/conversions where available, understanding commission and returns, and accounting for production costs, including credits. Avoid income promises.

It prohibits misleading product representations and unsupported effects. Product visuals must match the actual item. Recheck this policy for the student's market and publication date. [TikTok Shop AI requirements](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=491489038501663).

Reference pack: eligibility checklist with official links and checked date; product facts worksheet; claims/disclosure review; campaign plan; commission and cost worksheet.

## Appendix B — Voice route card

**Why the voice doesn't stick:** Soul IDs and Elements carry only the look. Video models such as Seedance 2.5 switch native audio on or off and invent a voice; they cannot select a saved Voice, even when driven from Claude. The voice travels as an audio file or is swapped in afterward.

| Route | Steps | Best for | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B: video first, swap voice** | 1) Generate the clip with native audio and dialogue in the prompt. 2) Run Voice Change with your saved Voice. 3) Check that the mouth movement still looks natural. | First talking clips; rescuing a clip you like | Basic |
| **A: voice first, audio reference** | 1) Generate the line in Seed Audio with your Voice (~0.5 credits). 2) Seedance 2.5 omni-reference: character Element as image reference, the line as audio reference. 3) Review the lip sync and voice match. | Seedance-quality acting with a consistent voice (test first) | Advanced |
| **C: silent picture + exact voice + lip sync** | 1) Generate the shot with audio off (cheaper). 2) Generate the exact line in your Voice. 3) Sync Lipsync 3 / Lipsync Studio. 4) Resolve length mismatches (loop, bounce, cut off, pad, retime). | Word-for-word scripts, ads, tutorials, multi-line scenes | Advanced |

Models that accept an audio reference: Seedance 2.5, Seedance 2.0, Wan 3.0 / 3.0 Prime, Wan 2.7, Wan 2.6, MiniMax H3 / H3 Max, Grok Video 1.5.

For two characters, give each an Element and a Voice, and shoot one speaker per shot. For other languages, finish in English, then use Dubbing. For narration, generate a voice-only track; no lip sync is needed.
