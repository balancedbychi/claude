## 1. Should the advanced material be an add-on? Recommendation: two tiers, no à-la-carte add-ons

**Recommendation:** Run **two monthly memberships** inside one Skool community: **AI Edit Basic** and **AI Edit Advanced**. Don't sell individual add-on courses yet.

**Why two tiers instead of add-ons:**
- **Simpler for members.** "Which of these seven add-ons do I need?" is the exact confusion your community exists to remove. Two doors are easy.
- **Simpler for you.** One upgrade path, one price change to manage, and no per-course access tracking.
- **The line is natural.** Basic = *learn to make one good thing by hand, on a budget.* Advanced = *build a system: automation, voice/dialogue production, specialist income tracks, and live review.*
- **Skool supports it.** Skool now allows multiple membership levels with different access and pricing inside one community, so both tiers share the same feed and members can see what they'd unlock.
- **No 1:1s needed.** The Advanced value comes from *group* reviews, deeper packs, and workshops, not your personal time.

### Pricing guidance (a starting point, not a rule)

Most paid Skool communities charge roughly **$27–$97/month**, with communities that include live calls near the upper-middle. A reasonable starting structure:

| Tier | Suggested launch price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | **$27–$39/month** | Keep it below one Higgsfield Plus month so it never feels like "another expensive AI course." |
| Advanced | **$67–$97/month** | Justified by specialist tracks, live reviews, and full project packs. |
| Founding offer (optional) | Lock a lower price for the first cohort | Rewards early members who help you find confusing steps. |
| Annual option | ~2 months free | Add once retention data exists. |

Be upfront in sales copy that **members pay for their own AI tools separately** (Higgsfield plan and optionally Claude/ChatGPT). Show the budget in section 5 so nobody is surprised.

### When to revisit
- Add **paid 1:1s or a small "Studio" cohort tier** only once Advanced has steady demand and you have more capacity.
- If one specialist track (e.g., UGC for brands) becomes much bigger than the others, it can later become its own **Pro track**. Don't do this at launch.

## 3. Foundation curriculum — changes to Modules 0–7

The module sequence stays the same. Here's what's added to each.

### Module 0 — Choose Your Creative Direction *(unchanged)*
Add a **budget field** to the direction brief: "My Higgsfield plan, credits per month, and my first-project budget (see section 5)."

### Module 1 — Understand AI Looks and Art Direction
Add: a **"look → model" crosswalk**, so members see which Higgsfield models tend to produce each look:
- Photorealistic / phone-camera UGC → Soul 2.0
- Cinematic → Cinema Studio Image 2.5, Soul Cinema
- Stylized / 3D / illustrated → Nano Banana Pro with style references; Grok Image or Z Image for bold, fast looks
- Editorial / studio / product → Soul 2.0, Marketing Studio Image

### Module 2 — Plan with ChatGPT *or* Claude, Organize with Claude Code
Changes:
- Rename to allow **either assistant** for planning, so members don't need two subscriptions.
- Add lesson **2.5: "Which assistant model for which job"**, taught from `05-claude-vs-chatgpt-model-guide.md` (fast / balanced / deep-thinking roles).
- Add a **"shot list with routes"** template: `shot | model | route (test / final) | voice route | est. credits`.
- Keep the manual (no-Claude-Code) route for Basic members.

### Module 3 — Higgsfield Foundations and Credit Control *(expanded to 6 lessons)*
1. The Higgsfield floor plan: website vs Supercomputer vs connector (overview only).
2. Navigating the interface, assets, settings, and usage history.
3. **The model ladder:** *test cheap → confirm → finish expensive.* Example: Kling 3.0 Turbo test (7.5 credits) → Kling 3.0 (10) → Seedance 2.5 final (35 at 5 s/720p; 120 at 10 s/1080p).
4. **The five cost levers:** model, duration, resolution, quality tier, audio on/off.
5. Running a deliberate test and logging it.
6. Keep, correct, simplify, switch, or stop.

Resources: model cheat sheet (from file 02), generation log, before-you-generate checklist.

### Module 4 — Build Your Character or AI Twin *(voice lesson expanded)*
Add:
- **4.3a Soul ID vs Elements:** when to train an identity (one person, Soul models) vs save an Element (works across image *and* video models, multiple characters per shot).
- **4.5 rewritten — "Your character is two assets: a look and a voice."** Create a Voice (your own or a permitted voice), write voice notes, and understand that **video models don't carry the voice automatically**.
- Assignment adds: Element ID and/or Soul ID, plus Voice ID, recorded in the character guide.

### Module 5 — Build Your Set
Add: **Soul Location** for empty-set references; save the approved set as an **Element (environment)** so it can be carried into video.

### Module 6 — Build and Manage Your Assets
Add to the asset record: **Higgsfield ID** (Element / Soul / Voice), **models tested with**, and **voice route**.

### Module 7 — Direct and Finish Your First Scene
Add:
- One line of dialogue using **Route B (generate → Voice Change)** as the Basic-tier method.
- Final review includes **credits per usable second** of finished video.

## 4. New: "Build Your System" modules (Advanced)

| Module | Course | Required member output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | **Claude + Higgsfield: The Studio Folder** | A working project folder with CLAUDE.md, manifest, shot list, and an auto-updated generation log |
| 9 | **Voice & Dialogue Production** | A two-character dialogue scene using Routes A and C, plus one dubbed version |
| 10 | **Supercomputer & Automation with Guardrails** | One installed Skill and one scheduled task with a written monthly credit cap and review routine |

### Module 8 — Claude + Higgsfield: The Studio Folder (5 lessons)
1. Connect Higgsfield to Claude (Settings → Connectors) and check your balance.
2. Build the Studio Folder and CLAUDE.md rules ("always show cost first").
3. Generate from your shot list: Claude reads the Element/Voice IDs and preflights cost.
4. Automatic logging: every generation becomes a row in your log.
5. Limits: Unlimited allowances generally don't apply to connector generations, spending instructions aren't hard caps, and you review everything.

### Module 9 — Voice & Dialogue Production (5 lessons)
1. Why the voice doesn't stick (the Seedance 2.5 lesson).
2. Route A: voice-first with an audio reference (Seedance 2.5 omni-reference).
3. Route C: silent picture + exact voice + Sync Lipsync.
4. Two characters, one speaker per shot; dialogue table.
5. Dubbing into a second language; disclosure and consent.

### Module 10 — Supercomputer & Automation (4 lessons)
1. What Supercomputer is (an agent, not hardware) and when to use it instead of Claude.
2. Skills Marketplace: install one Skill and read what it will generate and cost.
3. Scheduled tasks: set one up with a written monthly cap and a weekly review.
4. When *not* to automate: identity drift, unreviewed claims, and credit burn.

## 5. New: the first-project credit budget

Estimated using Higgsfield's preflight costs at default settings (checked Sept 23, 2026). Real costs vary by settings and plan.

| Step | Model | Quantity | Est. credits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Look exploration | Nano Banana 2 (1.5 each) | 10 | 15 |
| Character pack | Soul 2.0 (~1 each) | 20 | 20 |
| Set references | Nano Banana Pro (2 each) | 10 | 20 |
| Motion tests | Kling 3.0 Turbo, 5 s (7.5 each) | 6 | 45 |
| Final shots | Seedance 2.5, 5 s/720p (35 each) | 4 | 140 |
| Voice lines | Seed Audio (~0.5 each) | 10 | 5 |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 245 credits** |

That puts a finished 15–20-second first scene within reach of an entry-level Higgsfield plan. Plan prices and credit amounts differ between sources (for example, Starter at roughly 200–270 credits, Plus at 1,000–1,200 credits, and Ultra at 3,000 credits per month), so **confirm on Higgsfield's pricing page** and present the table as an estimate.

**Teaching point:** the same 4 final shots at 10 s/1080p on Seedance 2.5 would cost ~480 credits, about twice the whole project above. That one comparison teaches the whole cost-control module.
