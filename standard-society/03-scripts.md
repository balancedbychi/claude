# Script Series — Chi & Nia
22 scripts · The Standard Society · Instagram Reels

**Formats**
- **H** — Hero. Pinned. ~50s.
- **TH** — Two-hander. Nia + Chi. 30–40s. The core format.
- **N** — Nia solo. 15–25s. Dry, funny, forwardable. The reach engine.
- **C** — Chi solo. 18–25s. Balcony talking head. The save-and-follow engine.

Stage directions in *italics*. Every script ends with its comment keyword.

# H-1 · "The Ship"
**Pinned Reel · ~50s · Uses existing Titanic footage**

*Cut from the archive. No new Nia generation needed for the first two-thirds.*

**NIA** *(deck, grey dawn, blanket, notebook)*
> The ship was gorgeous.

*Cut — grand staircase, descending, one hand on the banister.*
> Heated pool. A gym. A café pretending to be in Paris.

*Cut — bow at dusk, glancing up at the crow's nest.*
> The lookouts didn't have binoculars. The key left the ship in someone's pocket. I'm sure it's fine.

*Cut — boat deck, night, tilted, breath fogging.*
> Seats for eleven hundred. Two thousand two hundred on board.

*Beat.*
> That was not in my notes.

*Hard cut — CHI, balcony, present day, full daylight. The tonal break is the point.*

**CHI**

*Beat. Direct to lens.*
> You weren't stupid. You were on a beautiful ship with bad information.

*Cut — NIA, present day, normal clothes, same yellow notebook, at a kitchen table.*

**NIA**
> So now I write it down. While I'm still on the boat.

**CHI**
> That's what this is. We're not here to make you cold.
>
> We're here to make you honest — early.

**END CARD:** *The Standard Society · Your needs matter*

**Caption:** Start here. 🚢📓

# PILLAR 1 — The urge to text, check, reconnect

## TH-1 · "The Draft"
*Nia: car, parked, night, phone lit. Chi: balcony, day.*

**NIA**
> It's 11:40. I have typed this message four times and deleted it four times. Current draft says "hey, hope you're good."
>
> Three words. Two hours.

*Holds up notebook.*
> I wrote down what I actually wanted to say.

*Reads.*
> "I want you to miss me first."

**CHI**
> That's not a text. That's a need. And you're allowed to have it.
>
> But "hey, hope you're good" at 11:40 won't get it met. It'll just get you a reply that keeps you waiting again.

**NIA**
> …so what do I do with it.

**CHI**
> Hold it. Not forever. Just tonight.
>
> See if the wanting is about him, or about how quiet it is.

*Nia writes: "Hold it. Just tonight."*

**CTA:** If you're deciding whether to send it, you can talk it through with Let It Go first.
**Keyword:** **DRAFT**

# PILLAR 3 — Missing someone while remembering why it hurt

**CHI**

*Beat.*

*Leans in slightly.*
> You can grieve someone who was only sometimes good to you. That's not weakness. That's an accurate memory.

*Nia writes: "Real, but not reliable."*

# PILLAR 4 — Asking for what you need, without apologising

## TH-4 · "Sorry, I Know That's A Lot"
*Nia: bathroom mirror, getting ready. Chi: balcony.*

**NIA**
> I asked him to text me back within a day. A day. Twenty-four hours.

*Beat.*
> And then I apologised for asking. In the same message. "Sorry, I know that's a lot."

**CHI**
> It isn't a lot. And here's the part I really want you to hear —
>
> the apology didn't make it easier for him to say yes. It made it easier for him to say no.

*Beat.*
> When you shrink a need down to nothing, you've told him it's fine to treat it like nothing.

**NIA**
> So how do I say it without the sorry.

**CHI**
> "I need a reply within a day. Can you do that?"
>
> That's it. That's the whole sentence.

**CTA:** What's the ask you keep apologising for?
**Keyword:** **FLOOR**

# The template, for writing more

**Two-hander (30–40s)**

1. **Nia, 0–8s** — one specific, slightly embarrassing, true moment. Time of day, a number, an object. Never a general feeling.
2. **Nia, 8–12s** — the notebook line or the question she can't answer.
3. **Chi, 12–25s** — validate in one sentence. Reframe in one sentence. *One* action, small enough to do tonight.
4. **Nia, 25–32s** — writes the reframe down. Doesn't resolve. Doesn't thank her.
5. **CTA, 32–38s** — soft, fits the moment, matched to the keyword.

**The five checks before anything ships**
- Does it start at a recognisable moment, not a lesson?
- Is there exactly one useful idea?
- Would this help someone who never buys anything?
- Who does she send it to, and what does she type with it?
- Does it avoid promising an outcome? *(See guardrails.)*
