**Why two beats rather than one line.** "it's 11:40" is the recognisable moment the brief asks
scripts to open on. "you already know" is the useful thought. Splitting them makes the viewer
supply the middle themselves, which is what makes it land.

## Audio

Silent by design. Add a **trending audio in the app at post time** — quiet and sparse, no lyrics
competing with the text, nothing triumphant. The clip carries no dialogue, so any sound works and
it rides whatever is current. This is the main reason b-roll beats talking video for volume.

## What to watch

**Sends per reach** is the number that matters. This post's job is to be forwarded with "this is
literally me." Saves and DRAFT triggers are secondary; comments are the least interesting signal.

If sends underperform, the caption is the first thing to change — it is doing the forwarding work,
not the clip.

## What building this proved

**Text belongs in the edit, never in generation.** The hook can be rewritten in seconds for free.
Had it been burned at generation, every hook test would cost 36 credits.

**Silence is an asset.** No dialogue means no voice element, no lip-sync, no drift — and it rides
whatever audio is trending this week rather than ageing with a sound.

**One source clip carries several posts.** The same 4 seconds with different text is a different
post. FS-1 is "you already know"; the same footage under *"he's been typing for four minutes. four."*
is a second post at zero additional generation cost. **That is the real economics of the b-roll
library** — 18 clips is not 18 posts, it is 18 clips × the hook bank.
