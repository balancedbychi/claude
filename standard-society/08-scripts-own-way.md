# Pillar 8 — Raising Your Standards & Getting Out Of Your Own Way

The enemy moves. Pillars 1–7 are about him — his silence, his inconsistency, his return.
**This one is about the thing you do to yourself**, and it's the hardest pillar to write
without sliding into blame. The rule: *getting in your own way is a survival habit that
worked once.* Never "stop self-sabotaging." Always "you learned this, and you can put it down."

This is also the bridge to the future membership. Pillars 1–7 answer *"what do I do right now?"*
This one answers *"how do I keep choosing this?"* — so it carries interest, never enrollment.

Written for the new mix: carousels lead, 5-second clips carry volume, talking video is rationed.

# Voice notes for this pillar

**Always**
- Name the habit, then immediately say it made sense once
- Use "you learned this" — never "you do this"
- Let Nia be the one who admits it first; Chi never diagnoses her
- Keep the action tiny. One sentence. One ask. One thing said out loud.

**Never**
- "Stop self-sabotaging," "do the work," "heal yourself," "mindset"
- Any suggestion she's the reason he behaved badly — the pillars don't cancel each other out
- Making the future membership sound available. This pillar builds appetite; it never opens a door.

**The line that keeps it safe:** *You got in your own way because at some point, getting out of it wasn't safe.* If a script can't live alongside that sentence, it's blaming her, and it's cut.
