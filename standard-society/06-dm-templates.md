# Auto-DM Templates

Seven DMs, one per comment keyword. With the lead magnet cut, **these are the free thing** —
and the only conversion copy in the funnel. Every sale passes through one of them.

## Rules

- **Help in the first four lines.** Before any price, before any link. She should be able to close
  the DM having got something, and that has to be true or the whole brand promise is a bluff.
- **One offer line, at the end.** Never two. Never a second follow-up DM chasing the sale.
- **"$17 to get in, 100 replies to start."** That exact framing. Never put the 150-for-$17 pack
  in the same message — the top-up is better per-reply value and the comparison makes the entry
  look like the bad deal. Packs are a later conversation, with someone who has used their hundred.
- **No promises.** Not clarity, not healing, not an outcome. It's a place to think, that answers back.
- **Never sell into distress.** If someone replies with something genuinely painful, a person
  answers, and the answer contains no link.
- **SOUP carries no offer.** It invites conversation instead. One in seven keeps the account from
  reading like a vending machine.

## BOAT — the hero Reel

> The six lines, and what they actually mean:
>
> **"The ship was gorgeous."** → He was impressive. You weren't stupid.
> **"The maths was criminal."** → The effort never added up, and you knew.
> **"The lookouts don't have binoculars."** → You had the information. You looked away.
> **"Seats for eleven hundred, souls on board two thousand two hundred."** → The numbers were always there to count.
> **"That was not in my notes."** → He showed you who he was.
> **"Next time: dry land."** → The standard, learned the hard way.
>
> Which one landed? Reply and tell me — that's usually the one you're still in.
