# Character Bible — Chi & Nia
The Standard Society · Instagram

---

## Why two characters

One talking head gives advice. Two characters give a **relationship the viewer can stand inside**.

Nia is the person in it. Chi is the person on solid ground. The viewer is Nia — and Chi is the friend they wish was on the phone at 11:40pm. That gap is the whole product: *Let It Go* exists for the moment between the urge and the action, and this format dramatises that moment every single post.

Critically: **Chi never judges Nia, and Nia never gets fixed.** Nia keeps struggling across the series. That's the brand promise made visual — nobody gets shamed for staying, returning, or finding it hard.

- Makes it funny so it's bearable

**What Nia never does** — get fixed in one video, become a mouthpiece for the advice, apologise for feeling it, get mocked by the edit.

## The Titanic canon (your unfair advantage)

Nia's existing footage isn't a random old project. It's a ready-made origin myth that explains the entire brand in one watch:

| Her line | What it means here |
|---|---|
| "The ship was gorgeous." | He was impressive. You weren't stupid. |
| "The maths was criminal." | The effort never added up and you knew it. |
| "The lookouts don't have binoculars… I'm sure it's fine." | You had the information. You looked away. |
| "Seats for eleven hundred, souls on board two thousand two hundred." | The numbers were always there to count. |
| "That was not in my notes." | He showed you who he was. |
| "Next time: dry land. Only dry land." | The standard, learned the hard way. |

Use it as the **pinned Reel** (script H-1). Everything after it inherits the metaphor for free — which is why Nia can later say "beautiful ship, not enough lifeboats" in 8 seconds and the whole audience gets it.
