# Asset System

The format mix, what each format is for, and how the existing scripts re-cut into it.

---

## 1. The shift

The original plan was Reels-led: a talking video most days. **The new mix inverts that** —
stills and b-roll carry the volume, talking video becomes a weekly accent.

| Format | Per week | Length / size | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carousel** | 3 | 6–9 slides · 4:5 | Saves, profile visits, the teaching format |
| **5-second clip** | 4 | 5s · 9:16 · no speech | Reach and sends. Cheap volume. |
| **Talking video** | 1–2 | 15–20s · 9:16 | Character and trust. The face of the brand. |
| **Stories** | daily | 3–5 frames | Conversion. Where the link lives. |

Eight to nine feed posts a week, a bit over one a day.

**This is an upgrade, not a compromise.** Talking-head AI video is where every failure mode
lives: identity drift, lip-sync artefacts, the voice re-rolling, the half-second of uncanny that
makes someone scroll. Rationing it to one or two a week means each one gets the attention and the
re-rolls it deserves — while stills and b-roll, which have almost none of those failure modes,
do the heavy lifting.

**Three things it buys you:**

1. **You can start now.** Stills and b-roll carry no dialogue, so they need no voice element. Nia's
   clone is still pending and it no longer blocks a single post.
2. **Your script bank got three times longer.** At 1–2 talking videos a week, the existing Chi and
   Nia solos are a two-month supply instead of a two-week one.
3. **Consistency stops being a gamble.** A still either looks like her or it doesn't, and you can
   see it before you commit. A 20-second talking clip can drift halfway through.

## 2. Carousels

**4:5, 1080 × 1350.** Six to nine slides. Slide one is a cover, not a slide — it's the only thing
most people see, so it carries the hook and nothing else.

**Anatomy**

| Slide | Does |
|---|---|
| 1 | The hook. One line. Character portrait or a strong object shot. |
| 2 | Name the problem in her words. Often a flat colour, no image. |
| 3–7 | One idea per slide. Alternate image slides and flat-colour text slides so the swipe has rhythm. |
| 8 | The turn — the line worth screenshotting. Usually Chi. |
| 9 | The question. Keyword prompt. |

**Colour system** — Nia slides on camel/cream, Chi slides on navy. In a two-hander carousel the
background flips when the speaker flips, so the call-and-response is visible before it's read.
Text in the brand display face, generous margins, nothing within 80px of an edge (IG crops the
preview).

**Why carousels matter more here than on most accounts:** they're the highest-save format, and
saves are the signal that tells Instagram this is reference material worth resurfacing. This
audience saves things to re-read at 11:40pm. Build for that.

## 2b. Art direction — bright modern luxury

**The look is a contemporary luxury apartment in abundant daylight.** Not cosy, not rustic, not
moody. Bright, airy, expensive, uncluttered.

This is not decoration — it is the brand's argument made visual. **Her home already looks like
someone with standards lives there.** She has taste, money, a beautiful flat, and she is still
sitting at 11:40pm deciding whether to send a text. That gap *is* The Standard Society: the woman
who has raised her standards everywhere except in who she accepts. Shoot the flat like she's already
won, because in every other area she has.

**The spine — put this in every prompt**

> Bright high-key editorial interiors photograph in a contemporary luxury apartment. Abundant soft
> diffused daylight from a large window, airy and uncluttered with generous negative space. Palette
> of warm white, bone and soft sand with matte black and brushed steel accents. Crisp modern digital
> capture, bright exposure, soft shadows only, shallow depth of field. **Nothing worn, rustic, dim or
> vintage.** No people, no text, no captions, no watermark.

**Materials**

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Honed marble, micro-cement, pale ash, limestone | Oak, pine, reclaimed or distressed wood |
| Linen, bouclé, crisp white cotton | Knits, florals, anything fussy |
| Matte black, brushed steel, chrome | **Aged brass**, copper, bronze, patina |
| Warm white, bone, soft sand, pale camel | Taupe-brown, olive, mustard, burgundy |
| Floor-to-ceiling glass, sheer linen curtains | Net curtains, small windows, blinds |

**Words that quietly age a shot** — every one of these appeared in the first batch and every one was
a mistake: *battered, worn, cheap, pale oak, aged brass, dim, one lamp, falling into darkness, cold
grey, bare branches, quiet domestic realism, phone photograph.*

**Light is the biggest single lever.** "One warm lamp, the rest falling into darkness" reads as
kitchen-sink drama. "Abundant soft diffused daylight, soft shadows only" reads as a magazine. Same
objects, same composition, completely different class signal.

**Night shots may be dark — never dingy.** Luxury at night means floor-to-ceiling glass, a blurred
city skyline, crisp white linen and rich clean shadows. Not a dim bedroom with a lamp.

**Shoot it editorial, not snapshot.** "Phone photograph" invites snapshot quality. Say
*editorial interiors photograph* and *crisp modern digital capture* instead — the format still reads
as native to the feed because of the 9:16 crop and the subject, not because the image looks amateur.

## 3. The b-roll library

Five-second clips, no speech, no lip-sync. Build the library once and reuse each shot across
many posts with different text.

**Object and environment shots — the workhorses.** No character means no identity drift, no
re-rolls, cheapest generation, infinite reuse.

- Notebook on a kitchen table, pen writing, then stopping
- Notebook page torn out and folded
- Two columns down a page, one much shorter than the other
- Phone face-down on a duvet
- Phone screen lighting the dark, clock reading 11:40
- A cup of tea going cold, steam gone
- An empty chair at a kitchen table, morning light
- A door, morning light through the gap
- Curtains moving, low sun
- A plate pushed forward an inch on a restaurant table

**Nia shots** — warm, handheld, night or low light.

- Thumb hovering over send, screen glow on her face
- Walking away from camera, coat, streetlights
- Sitting in a parked car, dashboard glow
- Writing in the notebook, only hands and the page
- Bathroom mirror, getting ready, not looking at the lens

**Chi shots** — cool, daylight, locked off or barely moving.

- Balcony, looking out, not speaking
- Wind in her hair, turning slowly to camera
- Hands on the railing, skyline soft behind

**Rules**
- No speech, ever. These are silent under text and a trending audio.
- One line of text, on screen for the full five seconds. If it needs two lines, it's a carousel.
- Hold the shot. No cuts inside five seconds.
- Every fourth clip carries a comment keyword; the rest just give something away.

## 4. Talking videos

**15–20 seconds. One or two a week. 9:16.**

- Speech starts inside the first half-second. No dead air, no silent reaction shot.
- One idea. A 20-second video holds one, and the CTA.
- Roughly 50–60 words. Past that you're rushing her.
- Chi on the balcony, Nia in a real room. Never both in one clip.
- Captions burned in at the edit, never in generation — so you can change a hook without paying
  for a new render.
- **Budget 2–3 re-rolls each.** This is the format that gets the attention now, because there are
  only one or two of them.

## 5. Re-cutting the existing 22

The scripts don't get thrown away. Their delivery format changes, and most get *better* for it —
the two-handers were always call-and-response, which is what a carousel is.

| Original | Becomes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **H-1 The Ship** | Stays long-form, pinned | The one exception. It's the origin story and it needs the runtime. |
| **TH-1 … TH-7** (7 two-handers) | **Carousels**, 7–9 slides | Nia's lines on warm slides, Chi's on navy. The format was hiding in them already. |
| **C-1 … C-7** (Chi solos) | **Talking videos**, trim to 18s | Already the right shape. Cut one sentence from each. |
| **N-1 … N-7** (Nia solos) | Split: the longer four → **talking videos**; N-6, N-3 and the punchlines → **5s clips** | "Beautiful ship. Not enough lifeboats." is a five-second clip, not a fifteen-second one. |
| **Hook bank** (30 hooks) | **5s clip text** | Each hook is already one line on one shot. That's the whole format. |

At 1–2 talking videos a week, C-1…C-7 plus four Nia solos is **roughly eight weeks of talking
video** before you repeat. The 30 hooks plus the ten Pillar 8 clips is **ten weeks of 5s clips.**

## 6. Higgsfield routing

| Format | Model | Inputs | Voice needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carousel stills** | `nano_banana_pro` · `seedream_v4_5` | Element placeholders `<<<id>>>` — multiple per prompt | No |
| **5s b-roll (object)** | `seedance_2_0` | Text prompt, or image-to-video from an approved still | No |
| **5s b-roll (character)** | `seedance_2_0` | Nia or Chi Element, no dialogue in the prompt | No |
| **Talking video** | `seedance_2_5` | Element + `@Audio1` from `generate_audio` | **Yes** |
| **Hero** | `seedance_2_0` + archive | Titanic footage + two new present-day shots | Yes, for the new shots |

**Elements, not Soul, for stills.** Soul takes one `soul_id` per generation; Elements support
multiple `<<<uuid>>>` placeholders in one prompt, which is what you need for any frame with both
characters or a character plus a prop.

**Object b-roll is the cheapest thing you can make** and the most reusable. Generate the still
first, approve it, then animate it — that way you never pay for a five-second render of a
notebook that turned out to be the wrong yellow.

**Batch order for week one, now that voice isn't blocking:**

1. Object b-roll × 10 — no characters, no voice, nothing can drift
2. Carousel stills for CR-1, CR-2, CR-3 — Element-locked, check identity before animating anything
3. Nia and Chi character b-roll × 8 — silent, so still no voice needed
4. Talking videos — **only once `Nia's-voice` exists.** Chi's can start immediately; hers is already locked.

## 7. Aspect ratios and safe areas

| Where | Ratio | Pixels | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carousel | 4:5 | 1080 × 1350 | Nothing within 80px of any edge |
| Clips, talking video, Stories | 9:16 | 1080 × 1920 | Top ~15% and bottom ~20% are covered by UI |
| Grid preview | 1:1 crop of the 4:5 | — | Check the centre square reads before posting |

The grid rhythm still holds: Nia tiles warm, Chi tiles cool, the yellow notebook in roughly every
third tile. With stills leading, the grid is now a much bigger share of the first impression than
it was under a Reels-led plan — it's worth designing the next nine tiles as a set, not one at a time.
