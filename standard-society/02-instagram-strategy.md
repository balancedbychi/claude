# Instagram Strategy — The Standard Society

> **Positioning (confirmed):** The Standard Society is the audience-facing brand. It runs the account, holds the community, and sells the $17 *Let It Go Interactive Guide*. **Munch Studio is the production engine** — it gets a credit layer and a proof-of-tool track (§9), never the main feed.
>
> Everything below is written for the woman deciding whether to text him at 11:40pm. She is the reader of every caption, every DM, every Story frame. Creators are a secondary audience served quietly in §9, and no post is ever written for both at once.

---

## 1. The one-line strategy

**Nia is the reach. Chi is the reason. The DM is the shop.**

Nia's dry honesty gets shared. Chi's clarity gets saved and followed. The comment-to-DM automation turns both into a $17 sale without ever putting a hard pitch in a Reel.

---

## 2. Instagram is not TikTok — what changes

Your brief was built for TikTok's twice-daily, hook-and-churn rhythm. That rhythm doesn't transfer cleanly. Four things change:

| | TikTok | Instagram |
|---|---|---|
| **What drives reach** | Watch time and completion | **Sends per reach** — DM shares to one specific person |
| **Winning post shape** | Fast hook, single idea | Hook + a line worth *forwarding* |
| **Where money happens** | Bio link, weakly | **DMs and Stories**, strongly |
| **Post volume** | 2×/day rewarded | 2×/day splits reach unless the library is deep |

The single most important consequence: **write for the send, not the scroll.** A woman watching a Nia video doesn't think "that was good." She thinks *"this is literally me and Danielle"* and forwards it. Every two-hander script is built so there's a moment where the viewer sees her own group chat.

The practical test before anything ships: **"Who does she send this to, and what does she type with it?"** If you can't answer, it's a weak post.

## 3. Content pillars

Seven themes from the brief, mapped to formats and shares of the calendar.

| # | Pillar | Viewer's live thought | Lead | Format | Keyword | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The urge to reach out | "Should I text him?" | Two-hander | Carousel | `DRAFT` | 15% |
| 2 | Mixed signals | "He's so confusing." | Chi | Talking video | `TWICE` | 15% |
| 3 | Missing him anyway | "Why do I miss someone who hurt me?" | Nia | 5s clips | `TUESDAY` | 15% |
| 4 | Asking without apologising | "Am I asking for too much?" | Two-hander | Carousel | `FLOOR` | 12% |
| 5 | Holding the boundary | "He came back." | Two-hander | Carousel + Story | `DIFFERENT` | 13% |
| 6 | Actions over words | "But he says he loves me." | Chi | Carousel | `LIST` | 10% |
| 7 | Everyday standards | "Is this even a big deal?" | Nia | 5s clips | `SOUP` | 10% |
| 8 | **Getting out of your own way** | "Why do I keep doing this to myself?" | Both | Carousel + clips | `OWN` | 10% |

Pillars 7 and 8 are the bridge to the future membership: 1–6 answer *"what do I do right now?"*, while these two answer *"how do I keep choosing this?"* They're the smallest slices today and grow as launch approaches.

**Pillar 8 is the one to write carefully.** It's the only pillar where the obstacle is her rather than him, which puts it one bad sentence away from blaming her for staying. The governing rule is in `08-scripts-own-way.md`: *you got in your own way because at some point, getting out of it wasn't safe.* Any script that can't sit beside that sentence is cut.

## 4. The funnel

```
REEL (reach)
  └─ "Comment DRAFT and I'll send you the questions"
       └─ AUTO-DM: the help itself, then one line about Let It Go
            └─ LET IT GO — $17 · 100 replies to start
                 └─ Reply packs — 50/$7 · 150/$17
                      └─ BROADCAST CHANNEL
                           └─ Membership interest list
```

**Comment keywords** — one per pillar, so the automation routes cleanly and you can read intent from the keyword alone:

`DRAFT` (urge to text) · `TWICE` (mixed signals) · `TUESDAY` (missing him) · `FLOOR` (asking) · `DIFFERENT` (he came back) · `LIST` (actions vs words) · `SOUP` (everyday standards) · `OWN` (your own way) · `BOAT` (the hero)

**There is no lead magnet — the DM is the free thing.** No download, no landing page, no asset to build. The auto-DM opens with the actual help: the three questions, the two-column exercise, the one-sentence ask. She gets something usable in the first four lines, before any mention of a price.

This is better than a freebie, not a compromise. A PDF asks her to leave Instagram, open a file, and remember she has it — at 11:40pm, she won't. A DM that helps her right there costs nothing to make, can't go stale, and keeps the brief's promise that people get something useful even when they never buy. The seven templates are in `06-dm-templates.md`; they are now the highest-leverage copy in the whole funnel, since every sale passes through one.

**Stories are where the money is.** Reels get discovered; Stories convert. Daily: one poll or question sticker, one Nia moment, one link frame. The link frame only lands after the other two have earned it.

**Broadcast channel — "Notes from the Standard."** Free, owned, escapes the algorithm. This is the correct home for membership warming: you build genuine interest without ever implying enrollment is open. Weekly message, in Nia's voice, one page from the notebook.

## 5. Cadence

Stills and b-roll lead; talking video is a weekly accent. Full rationale and production routing in `07-asset-system.md`.

| | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Post** | Carousel | 5s clip | Talking video | Carousel | 5s clip | 5s clip | Carousel |
| **Second** | — | — | — | — | 5s clip | — | Talking video ¹ |
| **Stories** | 3–5 frames daily |||||||
| **Broadcast** | — | — | — | ✓ | — | — | — |

¹ *Only in weeks with something to push. Otherwise one talking video a week is plenty.*

**Nine posts a week:** 3 carousels, 4–5 five-second clips, 1–2 talking videos.

Two posts on the same day compete for the same audience inside 24 hours, and the second usually eats the first — so the doubles sit on Friday and Sunday, away from the carousels that need the reach. Keep the second post a five-second clip: cheap, forwardable, and no loss if it underperforms.

**Best posting windows for this audience:** 7–9am (the commute scroll) and 9–11pm (the checking-his-story hour). The 9–11pm slot is genuinely important — that's when the urge to text peaks, which is exactly the moment *Let It Go* is built for.

## 7. The first 30 days

**Week 1 — Establish the world.**
Open with the Ship hero (H-1). Everything else inherits its metaphor. Introduce Nia solo, then Chi solo, then the first two-hander. No selling. Get the comment keyword habit started early — the DM does the giving, so there's nothing to build first.

**Week 2 — Prove the format.**
Two-handers every other day. First *Let It Go* mentions, always as a soft CTA inside the moment, never a hard pitch. Start Stories polls to build the DM habit.

**Week 3 — Push.**
Double-post. Run the strongest performers from weeks 1–2 again as carousels (different format, same idea, new reach). First direct Story sequence for *Let It Go*. Launch the broadcast channel.

**Week 4 — Deepen and plant.**
Introduce pillar 7 (everyday standards) properly. This is where membership interest starts — "what's one standard you're ready to stop talking yourself out of?" — as conversation, never as a waitlist pitch. Recut the month's three best Reels with new hooks.

## 8. Metrics that actually matter

Track these weekly. Ignore the rest.

| Metric | Why | Target by week 4 |
|---|---|---|
| **Sends per reach** | The IG reach engine | > 1.5% |
| **Saves per reach** | Signals "I'll need this later" | > 2% |
| Comment keyword triggers | Top of funnel, by pillar | 50+/week |
| DM → checkout click rate | Automation copy quality | > 25% |
| Follower → broadcast opt-in | Membership interest, honestly measured | > 5% |
| Reply pack repurchase | Real product engagement | track from day 1 |

**The one to watch:** sends per reach, split by character. My expectation is Nia out-sends Chi roughly 2:1 while Chi out-saves Nia. If that holds, Nia leads reach and Chi leads conversion — and you weight the calendar accordingly. If it doesn't hold, the two-hander balance is wrong and needs re-cutting.

## 9. The Munch Studio layer

Same assets, second job. This runs quietly and costs almost nothing extra:

- **Credit, don't advertise.** A small "made with Munch Studio" on Reel covers and in the caption's last line. Presence, not a pitch.
- **One BTS post a week**, Stories only — the prompt next to the output, the character lock, the before/after. Creators eat this; the core audience scrolls past harmlessly.
- **The case study is the account itself.** Once there are real numbers, "we built a 6-figure content brand with two AI characters and no camera" is a far stronger Munch Studio ad than any feature demo. Bank the numbers now so the case study is available later.
- **Keep it off the main feed.** The woman deciding whether to text him at 11:40pm does not care what made the video, and reminding her it's AI breaks the thing that makes it work.

## 10. What I'd watch out for

**The AI reveal.** This content works because it feels like a friend. If the audience discovers the characters are AI *from a critic rather than from you*, it reads as deception about something emotionally intimate. Get ahead of it: decide now whether the account is openly AI-made. My recommendation — be quietly open. Put it in the bio link page, credit Munch Studio on covers, don't make it the story. An audience that knows from day one is fine with it; one that finds out in month six is not.

**Nia must not get fixed.** The temptation after ten videos is to let her graduate into a second Chi. Don't. The moment Nia stops struggling, the audience loses the character who is them.

**Don't let Chi become a therapist.** She's a friend with a clear head, not a clinician. Watch for clinical creep in the language — "attachment style," "trauma response," "dysregulated." See guardrails doc.

**Comment section moderation.** This audience will post genuinely painful things in your comments. Have a response template and a line for when something needs more than a comment reply. Decide who's watching this before week 1, not during it.
