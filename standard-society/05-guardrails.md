# Compliance & Voice Guardrails

Check every script against this before it ships. These come directly from the brief and they matter — this audience is vulnerable, and the offer makes claims easy to overstate.

---

## Hard stops on the offer

| Never say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| Any specific number of replies included in the **$17 purchase** | **Not yet confirmed.** Do not state it anywhere until it is. | "Access to Let It Go is $17." |
| "Unlimited" / "as much as you need" | The initial payment does not cover all future replies | "Reply packs are there if you want to keep going — 50 for $7, 150 for $17." |
| "Expires in 30 days" / any use window | There is no window | "Use them at your own pace." |
| "It's like therapy" / "your therapist in your pocket" | It is not therapy | "Somewhere to think it through." |
| "A coach will help you" / any implied human | It's AI | "An AI chat that talks it through with you." |
| "You'll finally get clarity" / "this will heal you" | No outcome promises | "A place to sort out what you actually want to do." |
| "He'll come back if you…" | No relationship-outcome promises. Ever. | *Nothing. Don't go near it.* |

**The reply-count rule is the one most likely to get broken by accident** — it's the natural thing to put in a caption. Confirm the number before it appears in a single asset, caption, DM template, or Story frame.

## The membership

Not named, not priced, not dated, not built. Until launch details exist:

- ✅ Invite conversation — *"What's one standard you're ready to stop talking yourself out of?"*
- ✅ Build the broadcast channel as a place to gather
- ❌ No waitlist language that implies a date
- ❌ No "join," "enroll," "doors open," "spots"
- ❌ Don't invent calls, courses, coaching, expert access, or community features

## Voice rules

**Always**
- Start at a recognisable moment, not a lesson
- Roughly sixth-grade reading level
- Warm, like a thoughtful friend; gentle humour is welcome
- One useful idea per post — useful even if they never buy
- "You can care about someone *and* take care of yourself" is the through-line

**Never**
- Shame anyone for staying, going back, or struggling to let go
- Claim to know what *he* is thinking, feeling, or planning
- Clinical language — attachment style, trauma response, dysregulated, narcissist, love-bombing, avoidant
- Gender stereotypes ("men are like…", "women always…")
- Assume every viewer is a woman dating a man — examples can be, the voice shouldn't
- "Should." Chi gives options, not orders.

---

## The line to watch in practice

Raising your standards means **noticing whether actions match words, asking for what matters, and deciding what you'll accept.**

It does **not** mean being perfect, going cold, playing games, or pretending not to care.

If a script drifts toward "don't let him treat you like that, block him, you're too good for him" — it's off-brand. That's the *revenge* version of this genre and it's everywhere. The Standard Society is the version where you still love him and choose yourself anyway. That's the harder, rarer, more useful thing, and it's the whole differentiator.

## Comment section

This audience will post real pain in your comments. Before week 1:

- Decide who reads comments and how often
- Have a warm holding reply ready for someone in distress
- Know your line for "this needs more support than a comment reply" — and have a real signpost ready, not a product link
- Never reply to distress with a purchase prompt. Ever.
